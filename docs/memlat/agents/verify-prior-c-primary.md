## Additional findings

- The '1.9 GHz lock' was an effective 1.80 GHz on the N90. In every N90 run taken at that setting (all day-3 host runs, the day-2 nVHE run, the guest runs) the fixed-cycle timings are 2.223 ns for an L1 load (4 cycles), 4.45 ns for integer div (8 cycles) and 1.67 ns for double mul (3 cycles), i.e. 0.5556 ns per cycle; at the 2.1 GHz setting the same quantities are 1.906 / 3.81 / 1.43 ns, and the N80 at 1.9 GHz gives 2.107 ns. Ratios between modes are unaffected, but N80-versus-N90 comparisons need a factor of 0.948.
- The fast state is not a page-size effect, but one number in this bundle is. Noisy and clean nVHE agree at 0.25-2 MB (7.95 / 19.05 / 21.10 vs 7.96 / 19.48 / 20.75 ns), where huge pages would remove about 3 ns of L1-TLB-miss cost. The first, unaligned day-1 host run shows exactly that signature (5.74 / 13.14 / 15.13 ns against 6.70-6.82 / 16.15-16.58 / 17.79-18.11 in the later runs at the same clock), and its 64 MB value of 133.95 ns equals the N80 huge-page level (134.6-134.9 ns) rather than the 4 KiB fast level (152.4-154.2 ns). No pre-masking N90 host data set with 4 KiB pages goes beyond 8 MB.
- The per-walk increment of protected mode is not constant. Divided by the N80 refill rate it is about 3.7 ns per TLB refill at 3-4 MB and about 7 ns from 8 MB (N90 times over N80 counts, two machines); in sequential access at 64 MB it is at most about 3-4 ns, and a constant 6.5 ns per walk is rejected by the stride-256 data (p = 0.002-0.006) and far from the stride-512 / 1024 data (+0.26 / +0.37 ns observed, +0.81 / +1.63 expected). An explanation offered to the reviewer should not present the sequential null result as a simple one-sixteenth dilution of the random-access cost.
- By permutation test on means the paper's own statistic for 64 MB random access (+3.25%) has p = 0.09 in the full-suite data (medians p = 0.03, rank test p = 0.01; standalone means p = 0.011), because both groups contain sporadic low iterations (kvmoff 168.7 / 176.3 / 176.5; protected 166.4 / 178.8). All six memory numbers in the paper's host paragraph (-1.08, +1.99, +3.55, +0.29, +3.25, +0.55%) reproduce as 10-run means from the four clean runs.
- The fast state is not uniformly faster on the host: in the noisy nVHE run stride-256 latency at 12-64 MB is 4.5-8.5% higher and bw_mem rd at 33.55 / 67.11 MB is 6-7% lower than in the clean run. N80 Exp BG shows that memory traffic from other cores at a fixed interconnect frequency slows 64 MB random access from 148-154 ns to 197 (one reader), 247 (one writer) and 335 ns (two writers), so 'noisy is faster' cannot come from background traffic as such.
- The paper.txt line numbers given in the claims' evidence are off by 5-6 lines: P147-P151 are at lines 214-218, P161 at 229, P167 at 235. Paragraph ids and quotations are correct.
- Claim 35 cites HEAD of drivers/devfreq/phytium_noc.c. The kernel that was measured (tag 6.6.0-73.76, build #76 of 2026-05-15) carries the older 443-line driver; the v1/v2 rewrite was committed on 2026-06-05. The behaviour relevant here is the same in both, but line numbers and register names differ, and the tag version's comment describes the load counter as read/write commands.
- experiments/memlat-probe/README.zh-CN.md states that the FTC862 PMU has no dtlb_walk event. The June N80 perf logs show that raw event 0x34 counts (r34 = 21,371 and 21,453, close to l2d_tlb_refill), so walk counts can be collected on this core.
- Host and guest data are kept apart in all fourteen claims. The only cross-use is the labelled comparison of the protected host with x-kernel guests at 64 MB in claims 32 and 40; it holds from 16 MB up, while at 3-12 MB the guests are 2-9 ns slower than the protected host, so the match is not a like-for-like identity.
