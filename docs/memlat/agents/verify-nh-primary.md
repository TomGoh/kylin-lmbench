## nh#3: partly-correct (confidence medium)

Claim under test: If the high 8 MB level on the N90 came from scattered frames (E2), and the 12 MB child took those frames back as every 12 MB child did on the N80 (Exp H7: 2033-2034 of the 8 MB child's 2049 frames in 4 of 4 sweeps; the same code at both kernel tags), then by Exp H8 the 12 MB point would read about 1.16-1.19 times the low level after nearly every high 8 MB point. So E2 cannot produce the isolated high 8 MB point unless the N90's allocator failed to hand the frames back in about four of five sweeps; and the hand-back is what E2 uses to explain the carry-over to the next point.

Statement that holds: Exp H7 on the N80: the 12 MB child received 2033-2034 of the 8 MB child's 2049 frames in 4 of 4 sweeps, in exactly reversed order. If the N90 high 8 MB level came from scattered frames (E2) and the N90 hand-back behaved the same way, then by Exp H8 the 12 MB point after a high 8 MB point would read about 1.16-1.19 times the low level at strides 128/256 (H8 has no other strides). If the 12 MB child's extra 4 MB were also scattered, as on the N80, it would read about 1.24-1.28. The N90 shows a 12 MB value above 1.12 after 19 of 93 high 8 MB points (about one fifth); the observed group is 1.22-1.30, with 7 values between 1.10 and 1.22. E2 therefore needs the 8-to-12 MB hand-back to fail in roughly four of five high sweeps on the N90. That has not been measured: no N90 frame data exist. ## Additional findings

- The N90 high 8 MB level is not a 'no-L3-hit' level: it lies about 20% ABOVE the 12-64 MB plateau of the same sweep. Examples: kvmoff iteration 2, stride 128, has 8 MB at 11.18 ns and 12 MB at 9.49 ns. The low 8 MB level is about at the plateau. Any explanation, E1 or E2, has to produce an 8 MB point slower than streaming from DRAM. E2 does this naturally only when the 8 MB child is scattered and the 12 MB child is not: on the N80, scattered 8 MB is 10.6 ns against a sorted 12 MB of 8.95 ns.
- The page-frame hand-back on the N80 is not uniformly reliable. In Exp H7 the hand-back into the 8 MB child (from the 6 MB child) returned only 98%/24%/21%/63% of frames, while every other transition from 1 MB up returned at least 83%. The frames that were not returned left cpu0's per-CPU list: they reappear only in the 48 and 64 MB children. Any argument that relies on the hand-back should cite this alongside the 8-to-12 MB result.
- Each child inherits the previous child's frames in exactly reversed virtual order (LIFO per-CPU list). A mechanism that depends on frame order would therefore alternate between successive children. On the N80 the order effect (reversed vs sorted, expL2-n80.log) is only +3% at 8 MB and +7% at 16 MB, too small to make the 20% level, but it has not been measured on the N90.
- Exp H8's head8 and shuffled8 layouts permute the same frames from 4 (respectively 33) THP blocks, so the L3 set and colour histogram and the DRAM placement are identical to sorted. H8 and H6 therefore test frame order and adjacency only, not which frames a buffer gets. Claims phrased as 'layout cannot cause X' should read 'frame order cannot cause X'.
- The non-quiesced nVHE 'fast state' run shows no high 8 MB point in 50 of 50 sweeps (strides 64-1024), and its sequential 12-64 MB values are 4-14% slower than quiesced, while its random-access values are 1.22-1.27 times faster. This is evidence that the random-access fast state and the sequential 8 MB two-level phenomenon are different things.
- The N90 high 8 MB state is strongly stride-dependent: 0/40 at strides 16 and 32, 4/40 at 64, then 28/22/17/22 at 128/256/512/1024. The 12 MB carry-over differs by boot: vhe 10/28 vs kvmoff 1/24.
- My scripts and outputs are in <scratch>/wf5/verify-nh-primary/: h8.py, h8shim.py, h7.py, n90.py, n90b.py, n90c.py, n90r.py, noisy.py, pct.py, pa73.c and pa79.c (page_alloc.c at both tags). I read no prior wf2 report on the nh claims; none exists.
