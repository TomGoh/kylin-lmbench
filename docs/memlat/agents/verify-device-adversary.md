## Additional findings

- Data kind, in answer to the requirement to keep host and guest experiments apart: all eight device claims are host-side measurements on the N80F3 booted in protected mode. None was taken on the N90 and none inside an x-kernel guest. Evidence that the probes ran on the bare host: all 732 pfn_min/pfn_max fields in the logs lie inside the host System RAM ranges of hw-survey-n80.txt:114-117, and the lowest PFN seen (0x84030) is the first host RAM page. Whether a VM was running at the same time is asserted only in the README; no raw log (for example a process list) shows it. Because no other boot mode was measured on the N80, none of these numbers is a protected-versus-baseline difference. They describe the workload and the machine, and cannot stand in for the N90 host data (Figure 7) or the guest data (Figure 8).
- Unexplained TLB anomaly that no claim covers: in 7 of the 30 sequential 64 MB runs of expB.log, L2 TLB refills are 1.7% to 6.2% below one per page crossing (ratios 0.9376, 0.9656, 0.9782, 0.9762, 0.9831, 0.9507, 0.9771), as are 2 of the 15 runs of expD.log. The shortfall in expB.log:6 is 1023 pages per lap, i.e. exactly 2 x 512 pages, which is what two 2 MiB mappings inside the buffer would produce, although the buffer has MADV_NOHUGEPAGE and the klinux source honours it. The cause is not identified. 'One walk per page crossing' is therefore the usual behaviour on this system, not an invariant.
- The N80 moved the probes' pages while they ran. In expL.log three runs show colour statistics and contiguity changing between layouts, and in expL.log:67-71 about 14% of adjacent pairs turn from ascending to descending, the signature of compaction migrating pages. System THP was 'always' with about 4 GB of AnonHugePages in use (hw-survey-n80.txt:75,79). contig_frac and the colour statistics in every probe log are read before the timed section, so they can be stale for the measurement itself; at roughly 3 affected runs in 27 this is small but not zero.
- Exp F shows a reproducible placement effect of 3.2-3.7% on the 64 MB random chase (scatter and region:1 at 160.2-161.1 ns against 154.5-157.2 ns for the other five frame sets, no overlap, tracking L2 cache refills per access). That is as large as the 3.25% difference the paper reports between boot modes on the N90. Any argument that physical frame placement cannot account for a difference of about 3% in random-access latency is not supported by the N80 data.
- Exp L does not identify a mechanism. Three facts constrain it: descending physical order performs like ascending order; at 8 MB the shuffled-minus-sorted gap per access is roughly constant across strides (0.70, 0.70, 0.89, 0.62 ns for strides 128, 256, 512, 1024), so it is not proportional to page crossings per access; and at 16-32 MB the L2 TLB, L2 cache and L3 refill counts are equal between layouts while cycles per access differ by 9-10%. A forward stream prefetcher continuing into the physically next page would not predict the first fact. Also, 'L3 refills differ by less than 0.01' is a weak bound at 6-12 MB: differences of 0.003-0.006 per access at roughly 100 ns per extra memory fetch amount to 0.3-0.6 ns, against gaps of 0.28-1.02 ns; only at 16 and 32 MB (0.0018 and -0.0004) can extra misses be excluded as the cause.
- Event 0x2a (l3d_cache_refill) does not behave like a clean last-level-cache miss count on this core. It is 0.41 and 0.62 per access for random working sets of 3 and 4 MB (expA.log) and 0.48 per access for the 4 MB sequential stride-256 run (expB.log median 0.4844), all well below the 8 MiB L3, and Exp L shows no layout effect at 4 MB despite that 0.48. Either the event counts more than demand misses or the L3 capacity usable by one core is much smaller than 8 MiB. Arguments that treat ev2a as 'fraction of accesses going to memory' need this caveat. The random-chase latency already rises from 21 ns at 2 MB to 29 ns at 3 MB and 37 ns at 4 MB.
- Relevant to the paper's '8 MB LLC boundary' point, which is lmbench sequential stride 256 (scripts/build-xlsx.py:222): on the N80 that workload already takes an L2 TLB refill on most page crossings. Median refills per page crossing in expB.log are 0.0003 at 4 MB, 0.765 at 6 MB, 0.855 at 8 MB (range 0.70-0.90, i.e. about 0.053 refills per access), 0.937 at 12 MB, 0.982 at 16 MB and 1.000 at 64 MB. Its run-to-run spread at 8 MB is 8.31-10.24 ns (CV 5.2%) depending on the physical layout the allocator happened to give, which is larger than the 3.55% effect under discussion.
- Differences between the N80 runs and the N90 host runs that limit transfer: kernel build 6.6.0-79 against 6.6.0-73; N80 booted with security=box lsm=ksaf,bpf audit=1 while the N90 runs used lsm= audit=0 (results/n90-day3-host/*-summary.txt); system THP 'always' on the N80 (probes use MADV_NOHUGEPAGE); desktop session running with load 0.3-2.0 (meta.log); cpu3 and cpu7 at 2.9 GHz on the N80 while all eight N90 cores were capped at 1.9 GHz. The only primary evidence in the repository for the N90 core type is one guest boot log line (results/n90-day1/guest-N5-bootlog.txt:1, MIDR 0x700f8620); the N90 summaries record neither cache geometry nor the interconnect governor. The N80 trans_stat shows that under the driver-default simple_ondemand governor the interconnect spent most of its first 16 s below 1800 MHz (9.4 s at 600 MHz), so the unrecorded N90 interconnect state is a live uncontrolled variable when N80 and N90 latencies are compared.
- Estimator and fidelity notes: every latency in these claims is ns_med, the median of 11 repetitions of about 120 ms, whereas lmbench reports the minimum. With ns_min the Exp C figures become 82.5-84.2 ns against 103.3 ns (8 MB) and 137.8 against 155.3 ns (64 MB); the Exp L percentages change by at most 0.2 points; the Exp F maximum difference becomes 3.82%. probe.c's permutation() is a Fisher-Yates shuffle rather than a line-by-line copy of lmbench's (src/lib_timing.c:1632-1654), which does not change the page-crossing statistics but makes the README's 'copied line by line' inexact.
