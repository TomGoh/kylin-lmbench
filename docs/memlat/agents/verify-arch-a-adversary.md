## arch-literature#6: partly-correct (confidence medium)

Claim under test: Item 1f (size of a combined TLB entry). The Arm ARM does not dictate the size at which a TLB stores an entry (it explicitly mentions splintering a page and only requires invalidation to cover at least the size in the translation table entry). A single combined VA->PA entry can be linear only where both stages are single leaf mappings, so it covers at most min(stage-1 size, stage-2 size), further limited to sizes the TLB supports. Consequence for the pKVM host: 4 KiB stage-1 pages over 1 GiB stage-2 blocks give 4 KiB combined entries, i.e. the same TLB reach as native; reach is lost only where stage 2 is finer than stage 1 (for example kernel block mappings over stage-2 regions shattered to 4 KiB).

Statement that holds: Item 1f (size of a combined TLB entry). The Arm ARM does not dictate the size at which a TLB stores an entry: it mentions splintering a page when caching in the TLB (D5.10.1, p.D5-2638) and only requires invalidation to cover at least the size in the translation table entry (D5.10.2, p.D5-2646). A single combined VA->PA entry can cover at most min(stage-1 size, stage-2 size), further limited to the sizes the TLB supports; this is an inference supported by an Arm Ltd patent (US 10,649,907 B2), x86 practice (Bhargava et al.) and the Cortex-A53 TLB format, not an Arm ARM rule. For the pKVM host it follows that 4 KiB stage-1 pages over 1 GiB stage-2 blocks give 4 KiB combined entries, no smaller than native ones; entry size is lost only where stage 2 is finer than stage 1. This does not by itself establish the same TLB reach as native: whether stage-2-only (IPA->PA) and intermediate entries occupy TLB capacity is implementation specific (separate 64-entry IPA cache in Cortex-A53; one shared 1280-entry L2 TLB in Neoverse N1; shared TLB with 1.29-1.62x more misses on the Intel machine of Gandhi et al.) and is unknown for the FTC862.

Evidence: Documented inputs verified, both local and independent: page images D5-2638 ('as could occur from splintering a page when caching in the TLB') and D5-2646 ('does not dictate the form in which the TLB stores translation table entries ... must be at least the size that appears in the translation table entry'); the patent sentence was returned verbatim by my own fetch of patents.google.com/patent/US10649907B2/en (title 'Apparatus and method for handling page invalidate requests in an address translation cache', assignee ARM Ltd, granted 2020-05-12); Bhargava's splintering sentence is in my Columbia-hosted copy (lines 216 and 175); Cortex-A53 Table 6-13 fields 'S2 Level', 'S1 Size', 'Size' are in DDI 0500J (my re-fetch, same sha256 06004a00...) and in Arm-hosted DDI 0500D and 0500E. Against 'same TLB reach as native': (a) Arm Neoverse N1 TRM 100616_0301_01_en, A5.2.3 Table A5-4, fetched from documentation-service.arm.com: the 1280-entry L2 TLB stores VA to PA mappings, 'Intermediate physical address (IPA) to PA mappings for 2MB and 1GB ... block sizes' and 'Intermediate PAs obtained during a translation table walk' in one structure; (b) Gandhi et al. (in the investigator's own corpus, about line 663 of the text): TLB misses rose 1.29-1.62x because 'nested TLB entries (gPA->hPA) share the same physical structure as the normal TLB entries, reducing the effective capacity of the TLB'. The FTC862 TLB organisation is not public. The N90 host data show no sign of lost reach (the penalty in the 2-6 MB transition is smaller than a per-walk cost predicts, profile_fit.out), which supports the consequence without proving it.

## arch-literature#10: partly-correct (confidence medium)

(i) No caching at all: 9 references per TLB miss instead of 4, i.e. +5. With a stage-1 walk cache but no stage-2 caching: 3 instead of 1, i.e. +2. With a stage-1 walk cache that stores the physical address of the last-level table but no IPA->PA cache: 2 instead of 1, i.e. +1. (ii) Ideal walk caches (walk cache holding the PA plus an IPA->PA cache hit for the output address): 1 instead of 1, i.e. 0 extra. All extra reads go to the level-1 stage-2 table (8 KiB; at most about 32-34 entries in use on a 32 GiB machine), so each costs roughly one L1 data-cache hit. If stage 2 were 2 MiB blocks or 4 KiB pages the uncached extras would be +10 or +15.

Statement that holds: Item 5 derivation (arithmetic on the Arm ARM formula, conditional on its premises). For a host process with a 4-level stage 1 (4 KiB leaf) under a stage 2 that starts at level 1 (40-bit IPA, as the N90 boot log reports) and maps every address involved with 1 GiB blocks (allowed and preferred by the code, not measured on the N90), each stage-2 translation is one descriptor read. (i) No caching at all: 9 references per TLB miss instead of 4, i.e. +5. With a stage-1 walk cache that yields the IPA of the last-level table and no stage-2 caching: 3 instead of 1, i.e. +2. With a walk cache that stores the physical address of the last-level table but no IPA->PA cache: 2 instead of 1, i.e. +1. (ii) With that walk cache plus an IPA->PA cache hit for the output address: 1 instead of 1, i.e. 0 extra. Under this premise all extra reads go to the 8 KiB level-1 stage-2 table (RAM of a 32 GiB machine touches about 32 of its 1024 entries, about five cache lines); that each read then costs about one L1 data-cache hit (4 cycles, 2.2 ns for ordinary loads on the N90) is an assumption about the undocumented FTC862 walker. ## arch-literature#11: partly-correct (confidence high)

## arch-literature#12: partly-correct (confidence medium)

Claim under test: The measured penalty fits the two-stage-walk model but does not pin down the FTC862 walker. lmbench's random mode touches a different page on every access, so beyond TLB reach each access takes one TLB miss; +7.0 ns is then about 13 cycles per TLB miss, inside the architectural envelope of +1 to +5 extra descriptor reads (4 to 20 cycles at an assumed 4-cycle L1 hit) and most simply matched by 2 to 4 serialized extra reads, or fewer reads with a fixed per-step cost. It is not explained by lost TLB reach (combined entries stay 4 KiB) and not by memory-hierarchy latency (no penalty at 1 MB, no growth from 8 MB to 64 MB).

Statement that holds: The measured penalty is consistent with the two-stage-walk model but does not identify the FTC862 walker. lmbench's random mode moves to a different page on every access, and N80 protected-mode PMU counts give one second-level TLB refill (page walk) per access from 16 MB upward (0.006/0.447/0.967/1.000 at 1/4/8/16 MB); no such count exists for any baseline mode or for the N90. Read that way, the plateau of about +6.4 to +7.8 ns is the extra cost per page walk: about 12-14 cycles (12.7 at the N90's effective 1.8 GHz, 13.4 at the nominal 1.9 GHz), i.e. about 3.2 times the measured L1 load-to-use latency of 2.22 ns, inside the envelope of 0 to +5 extra descriptor reads for 1 GiB stage-2 blocks; which mix of reads and fixed per-step cost produces it is not determined. It cannot be a difference in the number of misses at 16-64 MB, where there is already one page walk per access, and the size profile follows the N80 page-walk rate (R2 0.96) better than the L3-refill rate (0.88) or a slowdown proportional to memory latency (0.84). At 2-6 MB, however, the penalty per page walk is only about 3.7-6.2 ns if the N80 rates apply, so a constant cost per miss is an approximation, and a fixed extra cost per L3 miss is not excluded by the size profile alone.

Evidence: lmbench: src/lib_mem.c:222-234 (comment quoted correctly) and 254-270 (page-aligned branch: one hop per page over a fixed random permutation of pages). N80 raw log results/memlat-n80-20260930/expA.log parsed by n80_expA.py; per hop, mean of 6 rows (3 seeds x 2 counter groups): ev2d (l2d_tlb_refill) 0.006 at 1 MB, 0.046 at 2, 0.190 at 3, 0.447 at 4, 0.838 at 6, 0.967 at 8, 0.998 at 12, 1.000 at 16-64 MB; ev2a (l3d_cache_refill) 0.06/0.41/0.62/0.79/0.88/0.93 at 2/3/4/6/8/12 MB and 0.97-1.00 above; ev5 (l1d_tlb_refill) 1.000 at every size. Protected mode only, N80, kernel 6.6.0-79. profile_fit.py (N90 Hodges-Lehmann penalty against N80 rates, least squares through the origin): page-walk rate c=6.93 ns, R2=0.962; L3-refill rate R2=0.882; proportional to (baseline latency - 20.7 ns) R2=0.842; penalty divided by page-walk rate: 4.0, 3.7, 3.8, 6.2 ns at 2, 3, 4, 6 MB and 6.4-7.8 ns from 8 MB. Cycles: 7.05 ns x 1.9 = 13.4; at the effective 1.8 GHz 12.7 (clock_check.py); 7.05 / 2.223 = 3.2 L1 latencies. 'Combined entries stay 4 KiB' is the inference of #6, not a measurement; the stronger argument is that the miss count cannot exceed one per access at 16-64 MB. The predictor and the response come from different machines and kernels, so the fit is indicative only. ## Additional findings

- Citation error in the evidence of #8: the alias table is in arch/arm64/kernel/pi/idreg-override.c:241-242 (HEAD and tag 6.6.0-73.76). The path arch/arm64/kernel/idreg-override.c does not exist in this tree. The strings are 'kvm_arm.mode=nvhe' and 'kvm_arm.mode=protected'; '-' is normalised to '_' at lines 352-353.
- Which EL2 code produced Figure 7: the raw lmbench headers on the N90 read 'Linux 6.6.0-73-generic #76-KYLINOS SMP Fri May 15 08:30:43 UTC 2026', i.e. the build of tag 6.6.0-73.76. At that tag CONFIG_X1_RMS defaults to n, no file in the tree sets it (the packaging builds from arch/arm64/configs/<flavour>_defconfig, debian/rules.d/2-binary-arch.mk:23), and the Rust sources were under arch/arm64/kvm/hyp/nvhe/rust/. CONFIG_X1_RMS=y entered kylinos_generic_defconfig with commit b1b2979f0c0bf (2026-06-29), first tag 6.6.0-76.85. Unless that build used a configuration not in the tree, the June host measurements ran the C pKVM EL2 code, not the Rust xhypervisor; the N80 of 2026-09-30 (6.6.0-79, CONFIG_X1_RMS=y) ran the Rust code. The hardware two-stage walk is the same in both, but a reply that attributes the host numbers to the Rust hypervisor should be checked against this. The internal note docs/n90-kvm-host/pkvm.md assumes Rust.
- Effective clock: the N90 runs labelled 1.9 GHz ran at about 1.8 GHz. lmbench's MHZ line is a static string in configs/CONFIG.host. In all four modes the L1 pointer-chase latency is 2.223 ns, float add 1.11, float mul 1.67, float div and integer div 4.45, double div 6.67 ns: within 0.014 cycles of integers at 1.80 GHz, 0.244 at 1.9 GHz (clock_check.py). The same machine gives 1.906 ns (4.00 cycles) when cpu_max_freqs is 2100000 (results/n90-day1, n90-day2), and the N80 gives 2.107 ns (4.00 cycles at 1.9 GHz; results/memlat-n80-20260930/expH-rand.log). So the FTC862 L1 load-to-use latency is 4 cycles, which supports the assumption in #10 and #12, and '+7 ns = 13.4 cycles at 1.9 GHz' should read about 12.7 cycles, or 3.2 L1 latencies.
- A second platform state exists in the host data. The non-quiesced nVHE run (n90-nvhe-noisy-full-cpu0.csv) is 17-35 ns faster in random access from 4 MB up (150.1 vs 185.0 ns at 64 MB, 83.9 vs 112.6 ns at 8 MB) while its sequential 64 MB latency is 8% slower. In every quiesced mode 2-3 of 10 iterations fall toward the fast values (kvmoff 12 MB iteration 2 = 121.44 ns vs 121.8 ns in the noisy run). docs/n90-kvm-host/pkvm.md reports that the first pkvm run (try1, dropped in commit 18e4c3a6, raw files not in the repository) stayed in the fast state from iteration 4 (154 ns at 64 MB). Neither THP nor devfreq state was recorded for the June runs. The pkvm penalty is a difference inside the slow state; the paper's mean-based +3.25% mixes both states. By the try1 medians quoted in that note, pkvm in the fast state is still 4-7 ns above the noisy nVHE run at 8-64 MB (91.0 vs 83.9; 154.4 vs 150.1), which I could not check against raw data.
- PARange belongs to the machine, not to the core type. A raw dmesg of another FTC862 machine (Great Wall D80F3, MIDR 0x700f8620, kernel 6.6.30+, under experiments/d3000-pkvm-apps/) prints 'IPA Size Limit: 48 bits', which gives a 4-level stage 2 starting at level 0; a 1 GiB block then costs two lookups (uncached +10). For the N90 the 40-bit value comes only from boot-log excerpts in docs/n90-kvm-host/{nvhe,vhe,pkvm}.md; the raw N80 survey also says 40 bits. The kernel has no Phytium-specific PARange handling (cpufeature.c, pi/idreg-override.c).
- 'No loss of TLB reach' (headline, #6, #12 and the Chinese paragraph in Table 12) is stronger than the evidence. In Arm's Neoverse N1 the 1280-entry L2 TLB also holds IPA->PA entries and intermediate walk results (TRM 100616_0301, Table A5-4), and Gandhi et al. measured 1.29-1.62x more TLB misses under nested paging because nested entries share the TLB. The N90 data show no such loss (the penalty in the 2-6 MB transition is below what a per-walk cost predicts), but no per-mode TLB counts exist.
- Wording in Table 12 and the headline. '1 MB（尚无 TLB 缺失）' is inaccurate: on the N80 the first-level data TLB misses on every access already at 0.5 MB (ev5 = 1.000 per hop; lmbench's tlb test reports 48 entries); what is absent at 1 MB is page-table walks (0.006 per access). 'Exactly that signature' and 'constant +7.0 ns' should be 'about 7 ns (5.3-8.4 ns depending on baseline and size)'; at 2-4 MB the penalty per page walk, using N80 rates, is about 3.7-4.0 ns. The rule that a combined TLB entry takes the smaller of the two sizes must not be presented as an Arm ARM rule.
- Two internal notes contradict the code. docs/n90-kvm-host/kvmoff.md says that with kvm-arm.mode=none the host kernel runs entirely at EL1; head.S:241 calls finalise_el2 unconditionally and hyp-stub.S:85-100 engages VHE unless id_aa64mmfr1.vh is overridden, so kvmoff is a VHE kernel at EL2 without KVM. docs/n90-kvm-host/vhe.md explains 'Malformed early option kvm-arm.mode' by repeated parameters; the real reason is that 'vhe' is not an accepted value (early_kvm_mode_cfg returns -EINVAL). Neither changes the single-stage conclusion.
- Arm ARM issue. All quotations were verified in DDI 0487E.a (July 2019) by reading page images of the archived PDF; the two central rules (table addresses are IPAs; (S1+1)*(S2+1)-1) are also in issue A.g, D4.2.4. The formula stands in an informative Note. I could not open the current issue (Arm's site is script-rendered and the PDF is too large), so the D5.x section numbers must be cited together with the issue 'E.a'; later issues renumber the chapter as D8.
- Minor points. The investigator's Table 5 gives the Mann-Whitney p at 8 MB as 2e-5; the exact two-sided value is 2/C(20,10) = 1.08e-5. The Simner et al. quotations come from the arXiv extended version (2203.00642v1), not the LNCS text. A second data set (results/standalone-mem-bench/, 10 runs per mode) reproduces the random-access difference (8 MB +7.1 to +7.4 ns, 64 MB +6.4 to +7.1 ns); whether it comes from separate boots is not recorded.
