## Additional findings

- Design flaw in Exp R that the claims do not mention: the rebuild flag is fully aliased with the physical direction of the buffer. Because each process reuses the previous process's frames in reverse order (the claim's own ev#3), rebuild=1 always ran on the mirror image of the rebuild=0 layout: descending at 16 and 64 MB (20/20 laps), ascending at 4, 6 and 8 MB (19/20). Any rebuild-against-no-rebuild difference in Exp R is therefore also an ascending-against-descending difference. Alternating the order of the two variants per lap would have removed this.
- A prediction written before Exp BG was refuted and this is not reported in the claims: worklog section 11.2 (commit 8b708298, 18:33:49) predicted 'sequential access slows, random access essentially unchanged'. Random access slowed by 30/63/122% at 64 MB and 87/124/222% at 8 MB.
- The N80's 8 MB stride-256 point is not single-valued as a property of the machine. With the interconnect pinned at 1800 MHz it took 8.37, 9.57 and 9.68 ns in three consecutive baseline runs of Exp BG (expBG.log:2,6,10) and 8.25 to 9.74 ns in the afternoon expB (n=30), a continuous layout-driven spread of 14 to 18%. The 12% rule used for the N90 flags 2 of 3 and 2 of 30 of these as 'high', so the rule cannot by itself tell a platform state from a layout effect. Exp R (adjacency 0.24-0.36 in all 40 runs) and Exp H (layout unrecorded) sampled only part of that range.
- On the N90 the 'high' level is drawn afresh for every measurement and is specific to the 8 MB point: within one lmbench run the 8 MB points of consecutive stride blocks, about 40 s apart, are unassociated (128 to 256: both 15, first only 13, second only 7, neither 5, phi -0.04; 256 to 512: phi +0.07); counts of high are 28/22/17/22 of 40 at strides 128/256/512/1024 at 8 MB, at most 6 of 40 at any other size, and 0 of 40 in the non-quiesced run. This supports the independence premise of ev#6 and means the state is not a slowly varying one; a per-allocation property (layout) and a governor sitting at a threshold would both behave this way.
- The N90 'fast state' is not uniformly faster. In the non-quiesced run large-block bandwidth is lower than in the quiesced nVHE run (bw_mem rd -5.9/-6.2/-7.1% and bzero -12.0/-12.3/-13.0% at 16.8/33.6/67.1 MB) and stride-256 sequential latency at 12 to 64 MB is 2 to 6% higher than the pooled quiesced median, while random access from 3 MB is 18 to 35% faster. H-i needs an extra assumption (contention from the non-quiesced services) for this, and Exp BG did not test loads of that size (its smallest load costs +47%).
- Kernel-source facts bearing on H-i: drivers/devfreq/phytium_noc.c is present at both release tags with default governor simple_ondemand, 100 ms polling, up-threshold 80, down-differential 10; cur_freq is read back through the ACPI method PGCF, not from a cached value. On the N80 the desktop power-manager policy table (devfreq-survey-n80.txt) lists only simple_ondemand and powersave for devfreq, so which agent set 'performance' 16.5 s after the driver loaded is unrecorded. Whether the module was loaded and which governor was active on the N90 in June is unrecorded.
- Estimator not stated in the claims: the 'medians' of ev#1 to ev#4 are medians over processes of ns_min, the fastest of 11 intervals, which matches lmbench's save_minimum (src/lat_mem_rd.c:148). Using ns_med changes the values by at most 0.06 ns and no conclusion.
