## Additional findings

- Session selection governs every session-level statement in this bundle. Ten N90 host sessions are documented; the analysis keeps eight. The two left out both sat in the fast state: the extra nVHE session (raw data present, 64 MB random median 150.1 ns) and the first pkvm run (documented median 154.4 ns; raw files deleted in commit 18e4c3a6 as a 'one-time stochastic outlier'). The kargs repeat counter in the summary files shows exactly one unrecorded deployment between the extra nVHE session (counter 4, 3 June 16:17) and the vhe session (counter 6, 22:20), which fits the deleted pkvm run; the two fast-state sessions were therefore consecutive on the same afternoon and evening.
- One boot session per configuration per campaign means iteration-level p-values compare sessions, not KVM modes. Two sessions without host stage-2 already differ at p = 5e-8 (vhe vs kvmoff, sequential 64 MB stride 1024, +5.4 %), p = 1.4e-7 (stride 512) and p = 0.008 (minor page fault, +0.95 %). Any iteration-level p-value for pkvm in the claims should be read against that background.
- The sporadic fast iterations in the clean sessions are size-restricted, which the claims do not state: 0 of 160 random-access cells at 3-8 MB and 0 of 240 sequential cells at 3-6 MB (strides 128 and 256) show the fast level, against 35 of 240 random cells at 12-64 MB (Fisher p = 6.5e-9). lmbench reports the minimum of 11 repetitions of at least 0.1 s each (src/lat_mem_rd.c:143-148, src/lib_timing.c:173-179, src/bench.h:198), and the 3-8 MB points take roughly one fifth of the time spent at 12-64 MB, so a state switching at random times would have produced about 7 fast cells at 3-8 MB (probability of none about 1e-3, my estimate). The sporadic events therefore depend on the workload or on its duration; only the whole-session cases (the extra nVHE session, and by documentation the first pkvm run) show the state from 3 MB upward.
- The rate of fast outliers differs between sessions: kvmoff has 16 of 60 fast cells at 12-64 MB, pkvm 5 of 60 (Fisher p = 0.015; kvmoff against the other three pooled, 20 of 180, p = 0.006). This inflates mean-based comparisons, which is the paper's estimator: pkvm minus kvmoff by means is +6.8, +10.9, +7.2, +9.0, +7.1 % at 12, 16, 24, 32, 48 MB against +4.5, +4.6, +4.7, +4.5, +3.8 % by medians.
- Cross-check mixing N90 host latencies with N80 counter data (same core type, different machine, labelled as such): the fast-state gap divided by L3 refills per access is 24-35 ns at every size from 3 to 64 MB, and L3 refills already reach 0.41 per access at 3 MB and 0.63 at 4 MB (results/memlat-n80-20260930/expA.log, field ev2a). The fast state thus behaves like a shorter DRAM access and begins where L3 misses begin, which explains why it shows no effect up to 2 MB; it is not a translation effect. The pkvm increment follows neither L3 refills nor, with a constant factor, TLB refills.
- Guest-data side check, done only to test the Figure 8 signature quoted in claim 19: the pVM guest series matches the host fast state in absolute values as well as in shape. pVM guest medians at 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64 MB are 129.5, 141.2, 147.2, 150.2, 151.1, 152.9 ns; the documented medians of the first pkvm host run are 128.1, 140.4, 147.4, 150.2, 151.2, 154.4 ns. The three other guest series sit at 164-193 ns.
- The extra nVHE session is not a same-condition counter-example. Besides the fast state it shows lat_tcp +24.9 % and lat_udp +25.4 % against clean nvhe, memory-load parallelism -17 to -27 % at 4-33 MB, an extra tracefs mount in its report header and about 30 times more wlan traffic at start; the documents say pvm-manage and AI services were running (docs/n90-kvm-host/SUMMARY.md:296-297). Whether a virtual machine was running on that host is not recorded.
- Provenance gaps: the standalone logs contain no timestamp, uptime, kernel or command line, and scripts/standalone-mem-bench.sh, which produced them, is not in the repository. That the four standalone sessions were separate boots follows only from the procedure documented at docs/n90-kvm-host/standalone-memory-bench-validation.md:80-81 and from the kargs counter (8 for the nvhe session at 02:02, 13 for pkvm try2 at 12:05, i.e. four deployments in between). THP=never and the masked services are asserted by procedure (prepare-host.sh, verify-clean-env.sh); no output of those checks is stored with the results.
- Framing: the claims treat kvmoff, nvhe and vhe as interchangeable replicates of 'no host stage-2'. The random-access data support that (session medians within 1 ns at 64 MB). The sequential data do not: the CONFIG.host vhe session is 3.6-5.4 % slower than kvmoff at strides 512-1024 and 3.9-4.1 % at stride 256, and that excess is not reproduced in the standalone vhe session. Pooling the three as one baseline is safe for random access only.
