## Additional findings

- Claim #8's 'DRAM line-throughput limit' is contradicted inside the same data, and this matters for how the paper's 'sequential 64 MB +0.29%' sentence may be explained. Sequential lmbench rows at stride >= 128 (all Figure-7 sequential rows use stride 256) are limited by prefetch reach, not by memory bandwidth: N80 expD shows 0.225 L3 refills per ns at stride 64 against 0.084-0.106 at strides 128-1024, and the N90 kvmoff boot shows 4.22 ns per new line at stride 64 against 9.0-10.5 ns at strides 128-1024.
- In the -t test the physical frames are allocated in chain order (first-touch order equals chain order, claims #3 and #6). The chain is therefore random in virtual address but follows whatever order the page allocator hands out frames, which can be near-monotonic in physical address. Stage-1 and stage-2 walk locality can differ for this reason. Nothing measures it: the N90 has no pagemap data, and in the N80 logs contig_frac is computed only for sequential runs (probe.c: 'if (!rnd) ... contig++'), so every random row prints contig_frac=0.0000 by construction and must not be read as 'no physical adjacency'.
- The N80 probe is not a line-for-line copy of lmbench in two respects. probe.c uses a Fisher-Yates shuffle, whereas lmbench's permutation() is the biased swap-with-any-index shuffle; my emulation, with exact glibc rand() and also with a uniform generator, shows the used pages skewed towards the start of the buffer (15.3% in the first eighth, 9.6% in the last, for 4-8 MiB runs). The probe also builds the chain once, whereas lmbench rewrites it before every timed interval. Neither changes per-hop event counts materially.
- The Figure-7 pkvm data differ from the other three configurations in more than the boot. vhe, kvmoff and nvhe ran back to back from 22:20 to 03:45; pkvm-try2 ran 8 h 20 min later (12:05-13:47), after five further kernel-argument changes (13 'quiet splash' groups in its recorded cmdline against 8 for the nvhe run). It is the second attempt: try1 was discarded and is not in the repo (scripts/build-xlsx-mean.py:32 calls try2 'representative'). Earlier 5-iteration kvmoff and vhe runs of 2026-06-03 were overwritten; only their summary files remain.
- The project's own documents disagree on the noise-control state: apply-masks.sh lists 90 + 12 units, the reports and the xlsx README say 72 + 12, QUICK-START says 84, and docs/n90-kvm-host/README.md section 4.8 lists 28. apply-masks.sh and verify-clean-env.sh were committed only after all runs. The mask list includes upower and the display manager, so masking may also have changed which component owns the NoC devfreq governor; on the N80 the desktop power manager had set it to 'performance'. This is a hypothesis, and no N90 artifact can decide it.
- Observation on the non-quiesced nVHE boot, relevant to the 'fast state' discussed in claims outside this bundle. Compared with the clean nVHE boot, random latency is 19-35% lower from 3 MiB up (64 MiB: 150.1 vs 185.0 ns; identical up to 2 MiB), while sequential rows at 12-64 MiB are 4-13% higher (64 MiB: stride 64 4.54 vs 4.23, stride 256 11.11 vs 10.24, stride 1024 11.92 vs 10.52 ns). bw_mem read and bzero at 64 MB are 7% and 13% lower, and par_mem at 32 MiB is 9.9 vs 11.9. The state therefore speeds up isolated one-miss-at-a-time accesses only, not streaming throughput. Any explanation (THP effectively on, NoC governor held high) has to reproduce both signs, and neither THP nor NoC state was recorded for any N90 boot.
- lmbench's parent process stays pinned to the same cpu0 as the measuring child and wakes once per second from select() (src/lib_timing.c:404-413) during the timed intervals. The effect is the same in every mode and negligible for 110 ms to 0.8 s intervals.
