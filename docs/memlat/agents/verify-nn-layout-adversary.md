## Additional findings

- H4's pre-registered prediction 'at 4 MB and below the three layouts differ by less than 2 percent' fails at the L2-cache capacity point: on the N80 the shuffled layouts and the natural run are 3.3, 4.9 and 7.9 percent slower than sorted at 0.25, 0.375 and 0.5 MB (at 0.5 MB sorted 3.540-3.549 ns in all four repetitions against 3.75-3.86 ns for the others); the workstation shows 3.7, 4.9 and 8.2 percent. Worklog 11.26 and claim nn#13 report this prediction as held. It does not touch the 8 MB result.
- Same-frames comparisons exist only where the design provides them. In thplayout (L2) sorted and shuffled are the same frames in one process. In layoutshim (H4, H5) every lat_mem_rd child builds its own pool, only adjacency is logged, and shuffled8 equals sorted in its set of frames only at multiples of 8 MB; at 2, 4, 6 and 12 MB the quoted ratios mix 'which frames' with 'order'. Wording such as 'depending only on the order of the same frames' (nn#17 a) should read 'of an equivalent set of frames (four whole huge pages of the process's pool)'.
- The N80 layout ratios were all measured in protected mode, while 30 of the 40 N90 values behind each level ratio come from boots without a host stage-2. On the N90 the level ratio is the same without and with stage-2 (strides 64 to 1024: non-protected boots pooled 1.234, 1.218, 1.205, 1.206, 1.132; protected boot 1.209, 1.228, 1.202, 1.198, 1.180). The only layout measurement without a host stage-2 is the workstation, which gives 1.076 at 8 MB and 1.04-1.06 at 16-64 MB for stride 256. Using the N80 ratio as E2's parameter-free prediction for the N90 therefore assumes that on the laptop platform the layout ratio does not depend on the host stage-2; this was not measured.
- At strides 512 and 1024 lmbench's layout ratio on the workstation (no host stage-2) is as large as on the N80: 8 MB 1.134 and 1.216 against 1.207 and 1.169; 16 MB 1.212 and 1.294 against 1.264 and 1.265; 4 MB 1.084 and 1.240 against 1.083 and 1.227. 'About a third of the N80's' (nn#8) therefore applies to stride 256 (and 64, 128) only, and at these two strides the effect is present inside the L3 cache (3-6 MB) on both machines.
- In absolute nanoseconds the N90 low level at 8 MB (4.234, 9.326, 10.323, 9.680, 10.848 for strides 64-1024) lies within 0-4 percent of the N80 shuffled level (4.357, 9.648, 10.619, 10.043, 10.844), and the N90 high level has no N80 counterpart. The E2 reading (N90 low = contiguous buffer) needs the N90 to be 16-20 percent slower than the N80 at equal layout (N90 low / N80 sorted 1.195, 1.162, 1.159, 1.163, 1.170; N90 high / N80 shuffled 1.186, 1.178, 1.173, 1.168, 1.163; the clock accounts for 5.6 percent; at 4 MB, where the N90 has one level, the factor is 1.14). Both readings fit the arithmetic, so the ratio agreement of nn#14 alone does not discriminate between a layout and a platform-state explanation.
- H5 was not a blind test of the ratio agreement: Exp Y (frames2, earlier the same evening) had already given 1.20, 1.19, 1.18, 1.18, 1.16 for strides 64-1024, and the N90 level ratios were tabulated in the worklog before H5 ran. H5 replicates the ratios with lmbench's own binary.
- README.md of results/memlat-n80-20260930 (line 97) says every program other than frames2 prints one line per process. thplayout (and layout) print one line per layout inside one process, which is the source of 'six processes per layout' in nn#8.
- binaries-n80.txt was written at 22:10, before thplayout.c (22:24) and layoutshim.c (23:04) existed, so no checksum ties the device copies of thplayout and layoutshim.so to the repository sources; the log formats match the sources.
- In H4, H5 and H6 the interconnect frequency was read only at the start and end of each sweep (governor 'performance', 1800000 each time), not sampled during the sweeps as in the evening's Exp H.
- On the N80 the 'aligned' layout of L2 removes all page walks yet changes the sequential figure by only 1.4 to 4.2 percent, and sorted and shuffled take the same number of walks; the 22-30 percent layout effect is therefore not an effect of the number of page walks.
