## Additional findings

- Unreported slot effect: GiB slots 0x22c0000000 and 0x2300000000 are slower than every other slot in the 32 MB random chase, in all 9 independent processes (Exp S reps 1-4, Exp V reps 1-4, V2): median ns_min +1.6 to +2.1 ns (about +3.5 cycles), per-slot medians 152.9 and 153.0 ns against 150.6 to 151.3 for the 12 others. In V2 the offset is absent at 3 and 4 MB (no walks) and present at every size from 6 MB. These are exactly the two slots with no guest pages, no RAM hole and no hypervisor pool, i.e. the only candidates for an intact 1 GiB stage-2 block. Cause unknown; it deserves a test, because a protected host without a VM (the N90 Figure 7 condition) would be mapped mostly by 1 GiB blocks.
- Exp S cannot compare 2 MiB against 1 GiB stage-2 blocks: at the time, 12 of the 14 slots were certainly not a single 1 GiB block (4 by memory layout, 8 by guest pages per vm-frames-n80.txt). The worklog statement in section 11.3 that the '2 MiB blocks cost more than 1 GiB blocks' expectation differs by less than 1 ns rests on a wrong grouping of slots.
- Kernel facts relevant to the whole bundle (tag 6.6.0-79.93, identical at 6.6.0-73.76 for pgtable.c and the Rust host.rs): the host stage-2 is prepopulated at init with the largest blocks, not built lazily; splitting a block refills the new table with next-level leaves (KVM_PGTABLE_S2_PREFAULT_BLOCK); when ownership returns to the host and a table holds no counted entries it is collapsed back to a block (stage2_coalesce_walk_table_post, pgtable.c:1127-1176). So a VM that has exited leaves no lasting 4 KiB split. CONFIG_X1_RMS is absent from arch/arm64/configs at tag 6.6.0-73.76, so the tree does not show whether the June N90 kernel used the Rust or the C EL2; both behave the same here.
- The per-walk cost from the random chase is not a constant: in the sequential chase of Exp W (one walk per 16 accesses, shuffled order, same slot) class shared costs only about 1 to 5 cycles per walk more than class clear, against 19 to 25 in the random chase at the same sizes. The figure should not be extrapolated to sequential numbers such as the reviewer's LLC-boundary point.
- V2 'cycles per walk' at 16 and 24 MB (35, 36) are inflated by an L3-refill difference between the classes (shared 1.000 against clear 0.94 to 0.97). Across Exp V and V2 the per-walk extra fits roughly 8 + 22 x (extra L2 refills per walk) cycles, within about 2.5 cycles.
- Near the L3 size the latency of the very same physical pages varies by up to 8 ns between passes (V2, 12 MB, slot 0x20c0000000 clear: 141.0 against 132.6 ns with L3 refills 0.973 against 0.904), and process to process by 6 ns (16 MB shared: 155.9 in Exp V against 162.1 in V2). Single-process comparisons at 6 to 16 MB are not reliable; the repository shows a repetition experiment (V3) queued, which is the right remedy.
- Archival gaps: guest-regions.txt (class list of Exp V) and vm-frames-n80-run12.txt (list regenerated for V2 and reused by W) are not in the results directory, so class membership cannot be re-derived. The VM's idleness is not recorded in any raw file. The VM footprint grows slowly (65249 pages / 386 regions at 19:24:38; 65261 / 391 in vm-frames-x.txt about half an hour later).
- Exp W 'sorted' and 'shuffled' are separate frames2 processes with separately allocated pools, unlike the afternoon Exp L, which reordered the same pages inside one process; the README and claim wording 'same page set' is approximate.
- scripts/apply-masks.sh entered the repository on 2026-06-04 20:41, about seven hours after the N90 protected-mode run of Figure 7 finished; the script version that actually ran is therefore not recorded either. The README line stating as fact that no VM ran on the N90 (results/memlat-n80-20260930/README.md:11) should carry the same 'by procedure only' qualifier as the claim.
