Against E2 in the N90 data: the high level is confined to the single 8 MB point: in the 90 sweeps at strides 128 to 1024 whose 8 MB point is high, the 12 MB point of the same sweep is at the low level in 67 (74 percent; median 1.008), whereas on the N80 the 12 MB child takes over 99 percent of the 8 MB child's frames in every sweep and is slow whenever the 8 MB child is; the level of the 8 MB point is independent from one stride to the next within a run (probability of high 0.51 after high, 0.50 after not high), whereas on the N80 the scattered state persisted in every sweep because the same frames recirculate; the frequency depends on the stride (1 of 40 at stride 32, where a 5 percent effect would be visible, 4 of 40 at stride 64, 17 to 29 of 40 at 128 to 1024); the high level is absent at 4 and 6 MB. Point (d) cannot be used for E2: 'about 1600 pages' is the floor of a self-tuned limit (nn#11, nn#12), and frame recycling predicts a carry-over to 12 MB that the N90 shows in only 22 percent of the cases. For E1: the quiesced N90 hosts do show a platform state with two levels that changes from one child to the next. In random access, which frame order does not affect (H6: at most 1.2 percent), 35 values at 12 to 64 MB in 22 of 40 iterations lie 3 to 21 percent below the usual level, the lowest at 0.79 to 0.80 of it, which is the level of the non-quiesced run; its size (1.23 to 1.27) is that of the two sequential levels (1.17 to 1.25). E1 explains the stride dependence naturally (strides 32 and 64 fetch about twice as many cache lines per second) but has never been measured at an interconnect frequency other than 1800 MHz and gives no prediction of the ratio. Judgement: not decided. E2 is sufficient in size, but the N90 pattern (isolated point, no carry-over, no persistence) is not what the allocator does on the N80, while a two-state platform behaviour of the right size is visible in the N90's own random-access data; the evidence does not support calling E2 the better supported explanation. Neither has been measured on the N90 (no frame numbers, no interconnect frequency recorded); the fast state of random access (18 to 35 percent) is a platform state of unknown cause. For the paper the conclusion is the same under both: +3.55 percent (stride 256, 8 MB: mean 11.600 against 11.203 ns) is the difference in how many of ten iterations fell on the high level (6 against 4); the same comparison gives -5.0, +2.8 and -1.8 percent at strides 128, 512 and 1024; it is not an overhead of protected mode.

## Additional findings

- Both kernel tags (6.6.0-73.76 for the June N90 runs, 6.6.0-79.93 for the N80) carry the self-tuning per-CPU list limit of mainline 6.7 (pcp->high between high_min and high_max, nr_pcp_alloc / nr_pcp_high / decay_pcp_high; 17 relevant functions of mm/page_alloc.c are byte-identical in the two tags). 'About 1600 pages' is therefore the floor of the limit, not a capacity. The claims nn#11, nn#12 and nn#17(d) in night2.json are the wording from before the analyst's own correction: worklog 11.29 / 11.30 and commits 5f09d5df (00:08) and e1d843c4 (00:16) reach the same conclusion from Exp H7.
- The repository changed while I was verifying: new commits appeared up to ff9bc482 (00:41) and the files expH7.log, expH7-frames.log and expH7-pfns.log (00:11) were added to results/memlat-n80-20260930. They are outside the list of logs named for this round; I used them as additional primary data and say so where I do. Worklog line numbers shift between commits, so I cite the predictions by commit (b88b579d, 4eeb2c79).
- The strongest observation against the layout explanation (E2) in the N90 data is not in the claim's list: the high level is an isolated 8 MB point. In 74 percent of the sweeps with a high 8 MB point the 12 MB point is at the low level, and the level at one stride is statistically independent of the level at the previous stride of the same run. On the N80, where layout was actually measured, a scattered 8 MB buffer is handed on almost entirely to the 12 MB child (99 percent of frames) and the scattered state persisted in every one of 32 sweeps (H2, H3, H7). A model of the kernel code with the N90's estimated limits gives the same hand-over. I found no layout mechanism that produces an isolated, non-persistent 8 MB point; a layout property that matters only at the cache boundary (for example page colours of the L3) was not tested and cannot be excluded.
- The quiesced N90 hosts show a two-state platform behaviour in their own random-access data: 35 values at 12 to 64 MB in 22 of 40 iterations are 3 to 21 percent faster than usual, the fastest equal to the non-quiesced run's level (e.g. 121.4 ns at 12 MB against 154.7 usual and 121.8 non-quiesced). Frame order cannot cause this (H6: at most 1.2 percent). The ratio of the two states (1.23 to 1.27) is the size of the two sequential levels. The claims treat the random-access fast state as a separate matter; it is direct evidence that a platform state of the E1 kind exists and changes from child to child on the quiesced N90.
- Cross-machine comparison (suggestive only, different machines and memory): the sequential latencies of the N90 non-quiesced run equal those of the N80 on scattered buffers with the interconnect fixed at 1800 MHz within 3 percent at 8 to 16 MB for all five strides (and within 2 percent of the N80 'natural' run from 8 to 64 MB at stride 256). N80 random access (protected, 1800 MHz) at 12 and 64 MB (128 and 154 ns) also equals the N90 fast state times the N90's protected-mode overhead (127 and 155 ns). This fits 'the non-quiesced N90 run is in the state the N80 was in that night', and it means the quiesced N90 low level at 8 MB (10.32 ns at stride 256) equals a scattered-buffer value at full interconnect speed (N80 natural 10.36) as well as a contiguous-buffer value on a platform about 1.16 times slower (N80 sorted 8.90). The data cannot tell these two readings apart.
- nn#16: the reduction of the coefficient of variation from 5.1 to 3.1 percent comes entirely from the 6 MB column. For the 35 cells at 8 MB and above, dividing by the N80 layout ratio increases the spread (2.6 to 3.2 percent), and at 12 MB and above the two tables are negatively correlated (-0.30).
- nn#10: prediction (b) could not be evaluated as worded, because no sweep had both probe3 runs at or below 0.3 adjacency (8 sweeps had both at or above 0.8). It fails in substance because lmbench's 8 MB values span only 4.2 percent. Prediction (a) rests on a single run in the low-adjacency group; against it the 12 descending-adjacent runs are 10.7 to 12.0 percent faster and the 11 ascending ones 14.2 to 15.3 percent. In H2 every 'pre' probe has ascending and every 'post' probe descending adjacency, so direction is confounded with position in the sequence.
- The N90 estimate in nn#12 depends on an unrecorded setting: with THP 'never' the floor of the per-CPU limit is about 1440 to 1555 pages (near the 6 MB child), with THP enabled about 2130 to 2160 (near the 8 MB child). THP 'never' on the N90 is known only from the procedure (scripts/prepare-host-v10-mmap.sh); nothing in results/n90-day3-host records it. On the N80 itself switching THP to 'never' would lower the floor from 1592 to 762 pages.
- The allocator model pcpsim.py in my directory is a reading aid, not a measurement: one zone, one migrate type, unmovable allocations ignored, one decay tick per second. Its outcome (flush at the 6 MB child's exit, scattered 8 MB child in every sweep) held in 5 of 6 parameter variants; in the sixth the 6 MB child stayed just under the floor and no flush occurred, as in the first sweep of H7.
