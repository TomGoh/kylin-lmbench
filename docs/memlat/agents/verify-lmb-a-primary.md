## Additional findings

- lmbench's permutation() (src/lib_timing.c:1648-1654) is the biased 'swap every i with a random position' shuffle, not a uniform one. In 30 runs of the original code, the first 2048 entries put 293/308/290/264/246/232/216/200 pages into the eight 8-MiB octants of the buffer (uniform would be 256 each). A -t run's pages therefore cover the whole 64 MiB but are about 1.5 times denser at low addresses than at the top; formulas that assume a uniform sample overstate PTE-line counts by about 0.5%.
- experiments/memlat-probe/probe.c is not a line-by-line copy of lib_mem.c, contrary to its README: it uses a true Fisher-Yates shuffle (probe.c:56-74), builds the chain once and never rebuilds it between the 11 repetitions, and continues the chase instead of restarting at the chain head. Per-hop TLB counts should be unaffected, but N80 timings are not exactly lmbench semantics.
- Sequential cost per new cache line depends strongly on stride. N90 kvmoff at 64 MiB: 4.22 ns per line at stride 64 against 9.0-10.5 ns at strides 128-1024. N80 expD: 0.225 L3 refills per ns at stride 64 against 0.084-0.106 at strides 128-1024. The Figure-7 rows 'LLC Bnd' and 'Main Mem' (stride 256) sit in the slow-stream regime, so they are not bounded by DRAM bandwidth.
- N80 event semantics are uncertain: at 4 MiB stride 256, 23 of 30 expB rows have no L3 colour above 16 pages, yet l3d_cache_refill is 0.48-0.49 per load, l2d_cache_refill only 0.009 per load, and l3d_cache accesses 1.9 per load. Treating l3d_cache_refill as 'lines fetched from DRAM' is unsafe on this core.
- Provenance of the host data: commit 18e4c3a6 says the raw reports were scrubbed of '[if: ...]' lines before committing, and that n90-pkvm-noLSM-try1 was excluded as a 'one-time stochastic outlier' with try2 as the 'accepted run'. `git log --all -- '*try1*'` is empty, so try1 cannot be inspected. pKVM is the only mode whose dataset is a re-run chosen after looking at the data. kvmoff and vhe also have earlier 5-iteration summaries (06-03 14:17 and 15:15) whose raw files were overwritten.
- Only scaling_max_freq is recorded for the host runs. The L1 plateau is 2.2237-2.2239 ns in all four configurations, which is 4.00 cycles at 1.799 GHz (4.22 cycles at 1.9 GHz), so the delivered clock was probably 1.8 GHz rather than the 1.9 GHz the task framing states.
- Indirect evidence that base pages were in effect: in all four Figure-7 boots (and the noisy boot) the random curve steps up between 48 and 64 pages (kvmoff 6.739 to 7.938 ns, nvhe 6.786 to 7.964, vhe 6.797 to 7.937, pkvm 6.774 to 7.923), matching lmbench's 'tlb: 48 pages'. With a THP-backed 64 MiB buffer, 64 pages would need at most 33 huge-page entries and no such step is expected. This is consistent with THP being off, not proof.
- The non-quiesced nVHE boot differs from the clean nVHE boot as a whole-boot memory-subsystem state, not as service noise: random latency is 18-35% lower from 3 MiB up, but sequential 64 MiB is 7-13% slower (strides 64-1024), bw_mem bzero at 16 MiB and above is 12-13% lower, par_mem at 4 MiB and above is 17-27% lower, and everything up to 2 MiB is unchanged. No recorded variable explains it; the 48-to-64-page step is still present, so it is not THP.
