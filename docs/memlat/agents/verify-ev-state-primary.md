## Additional findings

- Exp R is not a clean A/B test of the chain rewrite at 64 MB. The run order is fixed (rebuild=0, then rebuild=1 on the frames just freed), so rebuild is perfectly confounded with the direction of physical adjacency (20 of 20 ascending against 20 of 20 descending). The only rebuild=1 measurements with a mostly ascending layout (expBG.log:3, :7, :11) sit at the rebuild=0 level. Separating the two needs an ABBA order or a reversed layout in frames2/layout at 64 MB.
- Prediction record for the evening runs (worklog 11.2, unchanged since commit 8b708298): R had no prediction; H's 8 MB prediction passed; the BG prediction 'sequential slower, random access basically unchanged' failed for random access (+30% to +122% at 64 MB, +88% to +222% at 8 MB). No claim in this bundle reports that miss. The plan also named 3 full-command sweeps; 2 were run.
- The fast level does not require background services. In the quiesced N90 CSVs single random-access points reach it (kvmoff 32 MB iterations 1-2: 142 and 143 ns; kvmoff 12 MB 121 ns; vhe 24 MB 143 ns; pkvm 32 MB iteration 6: 151 ns), only at 12 MB and above and never at 8 MB or below (0 of 40 at each size). A June document (docs/n90-kvm-host/standalone-memory-bench-validation.md:359-372) further records that the quiesced pkvm 'try1' run switched from 194/179/194 to 154-155 ns at iteration 4 and stayed there for seven iterations; its raw files are not in the repository (0 files match try1), so this is unverifiable here, and the same passage lists try2 iteration 10 as 191 where the CSV has 179.
- Shape of the N90 slow/fast difference (non-quiesced against quiesced nVHE, my computation from the CSVs): no difference up to 2 MB (19.05 against 19.48 ns at 1 MB), then the latency excess over the 2 MB level is 3.0 times larger in the slow state at 3 MB, 2.8 at 4 MB, 1.7 at 6 MB, 1.5 at 8 MB and 1.27 from 24 MB. This excludes THP as the cause of the fast state (THP lowers the 0.5-2 MB points by about 20% on the N80: 15.28 against 18.82 ns at 1 MB) and excludes a constant penalty per memory access; it is what a load-following frequency state would produce, whether of the interconnect or of the DDR controller.
- The N80 Exp H curve is not uniformly at the N90 fast level: at 6 and 8 MB it is nearer the N90 quiesced curve. For sequential stride 256 at 64 MB the N80 (10.27 ns) matches the N90 quiesced hosts (pooled median 10.44) rather than the non-quiesced run (11.11). Statements of the form 'N80 equals the N90 fast state' need the size range and the access pattern attached.
- Program offset on the same machine and state: at 8 MB stride 256 lmbench's binary gives 10.16-10.51 ns (Exp H) where probe3 gives 9.22-9.43 ns (Exp R), about 11% apart. Probe values (ev#1 to ev#3) should not be compared in absolute terms with N90 lmbench values.
