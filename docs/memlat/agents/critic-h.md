## Must drop or soften

- **DOC-5.3-N90-states-are-NoC**: The document states as fact that the N90 slow state IS the NoC at low frequency and the fast state the NoC at high frequency. The N90 governor, its frequency, and whether the =m module was loaded were never recorded. The evidence is G5, one run on another machine (N80, protected, idle pVM). The N90 non-quiesced run also differs in a way NoC frequency does not explain: it is slower at sequential 12/16/64 MB (critic-g ng#3(5)). Safe wording: On the N80, random access under the traffic-following governor stays at the 600 MHz level. From 8 to 64 MB, its ratio to the 1800 MHz level matches the N90 'quiesced / fast-state' ratio within 0.02-0.035. At 4-6 MB it does not match (1.63 against 1.55, 1.60 against 1.38). This is consistent with the N90 states corresponding to low and high NoC frequency. The N90's NoC state was not recorded, so this is not confirmed on the N90.
- **DOC-1.4/1.5/4.3-most-likely + REPLY-181/183**: '最可能' / '主要是' / 'one-to-one' go beyond the evidence. Critic-g required the wording 'sufficient and leading candidate, not shown on the N90'. My re-check of G5 gives 11 of 13 points following the rule, not one-to-one and not 11 of 12. The 'judgeable' filter dropped only the one contradicting single-sample point. Safe wording: The NoC devfreq state is a sufficient and leading candidate for the N90's two 8 MB levels and its fast/slow states. It has not been shown to be the cause on the N90. On the N80 under simple_ondemand, 11 of 13 points at 8 MB matched whether the NoC reached 1800 MHz during the measurement (one run, Fisher p of about 0.03). With the NoC pinned at either end (4 points each), only one level appeared.
- **DOC-1.4/4.3-4-6MB-reproduced**: This uses a cross-machine baseline. Within the N90, quiesced/fast at 4/6 MB is only 1.08/1.11, against 1.16/1.25 on the N80. The N90 absolute values lie between the two N80 levels. Safe wording: On the N80 under traffic-following, sequential 4 and 6 MB are 16% and 25% slower (one level). The N90 quiesced runs also show one level there, but only 8% and 11% slower than the N90 fast state. That agrees in direction only.
- **DOC-4.3-ENOUGH-10ms**: This is refuted by src/lat_mem_rd.c:143 and src/lib_timing.c:1407-1410, which I verified. Safe wording: ENOUGH: 10000 in the N90 result headers does not shorten lat_mem_rd's timing. get_enough() takes the larger of ENOUGH and 100000 us.
- **REPLY-193-194-guest-NoC**: Mixes data kinds. It compares N80 host values (D) with N90 pVM guest values (G, Figure 8) and states the interconnect state of the guest run as fact. No host-side record of that run exists. The match holds only at 12 MB and above (at 4-8 MB: 33.8/60.4/100.9 against 36.0/55.3/97.9). Safe wording: This group of data was most likely collected in a different platform state. Its curve matches a host test whose background was not fully masked and that did not enable KylinX. On a host of the same platform family, with the interconnect pinned at its highest frequency, random-access latency at 12 MB and above is close to this group of data. The host-side state was not recorded at the time, so this cannot be fully confirmed.
- **REPLY-159/163-same-processor**: The workstation is a D3000 and the N80 a D3000M. Only the FTC862 core (same MIDR) and L1/L2 match. I checked hw-survey-n80.txt:28 and memlat-d80f3-20260930/README.md:13-16. - Workstation as a stage-2-free control. The task brief and the reply draft call it the 'same cores / same processor'. The final-reply audit shows the chip differs (D3000 against D3000M). In addition, the workstation README lists a different hierarchy below L2: a 4 MB L3 per 4 cores plus an 8 MB L4, against a single 8 MB L3 on the N80. The audits did not raise this for the LLC-boundary and layout comparisons. It means that any 8 MB workstation-against-N80 difference cannot be attributed to stage 2 alone.
- NoC attribution strength. Critic-g and the 1.4 bold sentence say 'sufficient and leading candidate'. The 1.4 heading, 4.3, 5.3 and reply lines 181/183 say 'most likely', 'is' or 'one-to-one'. The primary sources support only the former.

## Missing for root cause

- LLC boundary +3.55% (Figure 7, N90 host, 8 MB stride 256). The data support 'a count difference between two levels, not a protected-mode effect' (Fisher p 0.66; the levels are the same in all four modes; pkvm is below VHE). Missing before calling the cause of the levels a root cause:
(a) on the N90 itself, NoC governor and frequency samples taken during the 8 MB points, showing that the two levels track NoC frequency there;
(b) on one machine, interleaved and repeated boots of kvm-arm.mode=protected against none with the NoC pinned (performance, and separately powersave), showing that one level remains and that the pkvm-kvmoff difference vanishes;
(c) a formal test that +3.55% lies within the spread expected from binomial high-level counts out of 10;
(d) control or recording of buffer page layout, an independent factor of comparable size (about 13-19% on the N80);
(e) identification of the boot-time agent that sets the NoC governor, to know which state applied in each June boot;
(f) an explanation of the 3 of 40 intermediate N90 values (11.47, 11.73, 11.77), which a pure two-state NoC picture does not produce;
(g) an explanation of the slower large-size sequential reads in the N90 non-quiesced run;
(h) a repeat of G5 with the governor order randomised or reversed, since G5 is a single run in fixed order.
- 64 MB random +3.25% (Figure 7, N90 host). The stage-2 two-stage-walk explanation is a well-supported hypothesis. It has a robust median increment of +6.3 to 6.7 ns (+3.4-3.6%), zero increment up to 2 MB, and an onset that tracks walk counts on the N80. Missing:
(a) evidence that the NoC state was equal across the four June boots, or a re-measurement with it pinned. G5 shows the NoC moves this number by about 26%, and only indirect arguments exist against a NoC explanation (the quiesced runs already sit at the 600 MHz floor; powersave and ondemand differ by only about 1%; the increment's size profile peaks at 6 MB, not at 4 MB);
(b) a same-machine protected-against-none A/B (N90, or an N80 booted non-protected, which is a setting change for the user) with the NoC pinned, boots interleaved and repeated, measuring 64 MB random together with PMU 0x2d walks per hop and cycles per hop. It should show that the extra cost per hop equals walks per hop times the extra stage-2 walk cost;
(c) the stage-2 block size actually mapping the host's buffers, read from EL2 (1 GiB or 2 MiB or 4 KiB), to resolve the 3-4 cycle tension: the N90 increment matches the N80 2 MiB class, not the expected 1 GiB class;
(d) the June .config, confirming whether the Figure 7 protected boot ran the C or the Rust monitor;
(e) more than one recorded protected boot: the second is unstamped, and try1 was discarded;
(f) the L3-refill alternative (fits about as well as the walk count) excluded by a counter measurement in which walks and L3 refills are decoupled.
