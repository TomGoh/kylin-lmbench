## Unverified gaps

- No claim in this round got an 'unverifiable' vote. The open points inside the claims are the following.
- The N90's NoC devfreq governor, its frequency, and whether the phytium_noc module (=m) was loaded during the June 2026 host runs: none of this was recorded, so it cannot be decided from any available source.
- The identity of the agent that switched the N80 nocfreq governor to performance about 16.5 s after boot, and whether the same agent ran (or was masked by apply-masks.sh) on the N90 in June.
- Whether the N90 non-quiesced (fast) run was fast because of traffic-following or a pinned high governor. Its slower large-size sequential reads point to an extra factor (bandwidth contention or other state) that nothing measured.
- G5 is a single run with the governor states in fixed order (performance, ondemand, powersave) and no return to performance. The partial controls (alternating 600/1800 children inside ondemand; equal adjacency at 4-6 MB across states) reduce the order and layout confound but do not remove it.
- Beyond 8 MB, per-child NoC sample presence classifies the ondemand points only in a graded way (12 MB 12.07/12.33 with three 1800 samples each; 16 MB 11.67/11.92 with 4-5 samples). The 100 ms sampler cannot show whether a whole timed repetition ran at 1800 MHz.
- The adjacency explanation of the 10.71 ns 8 MB low point is post hoc, rests on one point, and was not predicted.
- The earlier audit cites the protected-mode N90 try1 run switching from slow to fast at iteration 4. Its raw files are absent, so this cannot be rechecked.

## Missing for root cause

- Both cited numbers reproduce as MEANS of 10 iterations, pkvm over kvmoff, from results/n90-day3-host: 8 MB sequential stride 256, 11.600/11.203 = +3.55%; 64 MB random, 188.13/182.21 = +3.25% (my script n90chk.py in <scratch>/wf6/completeness-critic). Both means are mixtures of NoC-like levels: the 8 MB values are bimodal, and the 64 MB random values include low outliers down to 159.9-168.7 ns in every mode. This is the starting point for both gaps below.
- LLC boundary (+3.55%), still missing: (a) a measurement on the N90 itself, not the N80, of the NoC governor and frequency during the 8 MB points, showing the two levels track NoC frequency there; (b) an A/B on one machine of kvm-arm.mode=protected vs none with the NoC governor pinned (performance, and separately powersave), boots interleaved and repeated, showing that the pkvm-kvmoff difference at 8 MB vanishes when the NoC is pinned; (c) a formal test that +3.55% falls within the spread expected from the count of high-level iterations out of 10 (the reading 'difference in how many of ten iterations fell on the high level' is plausible from the data: kvmoff 4 high/10, pkvm 5 high + 1 intermediate/10, but it has not been tested); (d) control or measurement of buffer page layout (adjacency), which is an independent factor of comparable size; (e) identification of the boot-time agent that sets the NoC governor, to know which governor applied in each June boot.
- 64 MB random (+3.25%), still missing: (a) the G5 round does not touch the stage-2 explanation. It shows instead that the NoC state shifts 64 MB random latency by about 26% (150.9 vs 190.7 on the N80), roughly eight times the cited effect, so the 3.25% cannot be attributed to stage-2 unless NoC frequency is shown equal or held fixed across the four boot modes; (b) a same-machine protected vs none A/B with the NoC pinned, interleaved boots, measuring 64 MB random together with PMU page-walk counts (0x2d) and cycles per hop, to show the extra cost per hop equals walks per hop times the extra two-stage walk cost; (c) on the N90 itself, or on an N80 booted into kvm-arm.mode=none (a setting change that needs the user), a non-protected baseline with the same kernel, THP setting and NoC state as the protected run (all N80 data so far are protected-only, and the N80 vs N90 comparisons mix machines and modes); (d) supporting but not decisive: the medians, which sit on the slow level, also show pkvm about 3.5% above kvmoff/nvhe (191.73 vs 185.05-185.43), consistent with a fixed per-walk overhead, but the NoC state of each boot remains unknown.
