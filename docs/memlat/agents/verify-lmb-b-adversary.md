## Additional findings

- Sequential rows are not free of a pkvm-vs-baseline gap, contrary to the framing of claim 25 and to the paper sentence built on one row (stride 256, 64 MiB, +0.29%): at stride 64 and 12-48 MiB all 10 pkvm runs lie above all 20 kvmoff and nvhe runs (+1.9..+2.7%, 4.45 vs 4.33 ns at 12 MiB), and pkvm is +2..+6% above them at strides 512-1024. It cannot be attributed to stage 2 because the boots order as kvmoff = nvhe < pkvm < vhe < nvhe-noisy (4.335 / 4.334 / 4.450 / 4.523 / 4.589 ns at stride 64, 12 MiB): five boots, four distinct levels, each with a within-boot spread of about 1%. Sequential DRAM-regime rows carry a boot-level offset of several percent that is larger than any pkvm effect.
- Every host run not taken under the full quiescing procedure (day 1, day 2, nvhe-noisy) is in the fast state of the random test, and every quiesced run (four Figure-7 boots, standalone logs) is in the normal state. The quiescing script masks upower and switches to multi-user.target (scripts/apply-masks.sh:57, :150-151; committed only after the runs), and the NoC devfreq driver of the measured kernel version defaults to simple_ondemand with 100 ms polling on peak bandwidth, upthreshold 80 (klinux 1e6773d13b560:drivers/devfreq/phytium_noc.c:289-290, 307, 344, 351-352). On the N80 the survey shows governor=performance with a desktop power manager running, and trans_stat shows 16.5 s of earlier ondemand operation, 9.4 s of it at 600 MHz. An interconnect-frequency state changed by the quiescing itself is therefore a live explanation for both the 8 MiB bimodality and the 185 vs 150 ns levels. It is not recorded for any N90 run and none of the verified claims excludes it.
- Newer N80 logs in results/memlat-n80-20260930 (expH-*.log, expR.log, expBG.log, written 18:45-19:15 on 2026-09-30 by another job, NoC sampled at 1800000 in all 17367 samples) bear on that alternative: real lat_mem_rd, stride 256, 8 MiB gives 10.16-10.51 ns in 16 of 16 sweeps (no slow state, RSD 0.8%) and random 64 MiB gives 152.4-154.2 ns with 4 KiB pages, i.e. the N90 fast-cluster and fast-state levels. With THP on, the N80 sequential latency is lower at every size from 4 to 64 MiB (8.95 vs 10.39 ns at 8 MiB, 9.58 vs 10.27 at 64 MiB), whereas the N90 fast-state boot is 4-8% slower than the clean boot at 12-64 MiB, so the THP candidate does not reproduce the sequential signature. Both comparisons are cross-machine and only suggestive.
- The fast/normal gap of the N90 random test (clean nVHE minus nvhe-noisy: 10.2 / 16.9 / 18.7 / 28.7 / 33.0 / 34.3 / 34.9 ns at 3 / 4 / 6 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 64 MiB) divided by the N80 l3d_cache_refill rate is 24-36 ns at every size, more uniform than per page walk (57 / 35 / 22 / 30 / 33 ns at 3 / 4 / 6 / 8 / 12 MiB). In the fast-state boot the large-block bandwidth rows are lower, not higher (bw_mem rd, bcopy, bzero at 16-64 MiB: -6..-13%) and par_mem at 16 MiB is 9.4 vs 12.4. The state looks like a cheaper isolated DRAM fetch, not like removed page walks and not like a uniformly faster memory path.
- Timing structure of the two states in the quiesced boots: the slow sequential state persists across consecutive children (up to seven in a row, about 9 s, in the vhe boot), and single fast children of the random test reach the full fast-state level only where a timed interval is short (12 MiB 121.4 vs 121.8 ns in the noisy boot; 16 MiB 132.4 and 134.9 vs 134.2; 24 MiB 143.1 vs 143.1; 48 MiB 148.5 vs 149.1) and never at 64 MiB, where one interval lasts 0.78 s (all 11 fast children there are intermediate, 159.9-178.8 vs 150.1). Both patterns fit a platform state that changes on a scale of fractions of a second to a minute better than a property fixed at process start.
- The N80 and the N90 differ in more than DIMMs. (a) Core clock at the same cpufreq setting: N80 1.90 GHz (PMU 1.898-1.906 cycles per ns; lmbench L1 2.107 ns), N90 1.80 GHz (L1 2.223 ns), so core-bound absolute numbers differ by 5.6% (sequential latency scales with the core clock up to 4 MiB: ratio 1.16 between the two N90 settings). (b) EL2 implementation: the N80 ran 6.6.0-79 with CONFIG_X1_RMS=y (Rust EL2), while a stock 6.6.0-73.76 build, as the N90 appears to have run, compiles the C EL2. Any N80 per-walk or per-load quantity used to explain the N90 'protected' numbers crosses both differences.
- The guest run that Figure 8 presents as the protected VM is identified as such only by its directory name; its own metadata carries ENV_TAG=xkernel-pkvm-np (results/xkernel-n90-pkvm-pvm-20260605-163040/started.txt and autorun.marker; already flagged in docs/vm-lmbench-methodology.zh-CN.md:266). It carries both signatures of the host fast state (random curve and 0 of 50 slow draws at 8 MiB), so the negative overhead in Figure 8 cannot be assigned to protected VMs.

Only at 64 MiB (3 against 2 fast runs) do mean and median agree (+3.25% vs +3.51%).
