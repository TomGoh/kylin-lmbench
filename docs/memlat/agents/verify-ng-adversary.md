## Additional findings

- Worklog 11.34 scores prediction 2 as 'held' although powersave 8 MB was 28% slower against a predicted 15-25%, and prediction 1 (performance 8 MB 10.1-10.5 ns) missed: the readings were 9.90-10.09 plus 9.08. The analyst notes both but still marks the predictions as held; the wording should say 'direction held, size outside the stated range'.
- Premise in the task brief (and a possible reader assumption) corrected: the timed part of a lat_mem_rd child is not ~0.1-0.2 s. lat_mem_rd.c:143 passes enough=100000 us; lib_timing.c benchmp_interval records 11 repetitions of >=0.95*enough each and the reported value is their minimum. The timed part is therefore about 1.1-1.2 s per child, about one 100 ms NoC sample per repetition. Consequence: one repetition at 1800 MHz is enough to pull a child to the low level, and a single 1800 sample cannot show whether a whole repetition ran at 1800 (sw6 high with 1 sample vs sw10 and the full command low with 1 sample).
- The premise that the 12-64 MB ondemand points read near the performance level 'although most samples are at 600 MHz' is false at 64 MB: every 64 MB child under ondemand had 2-9 samples at 1800 MHz and none at 600. At 12 MB the only child with no 1800 sample (sw11) read 13.42 ns, equal to powersave's 13.43, which supports the reading. Counter-examples remain at 12-16 MB: 12 MB sw4 12.07 and sw8 12.33 with 3 samples at 1800 each; 16 MB sw1 11.67 (5x1800) and sw8 11.92 (4x1800); 16 MB sw4 12.05 with no 1800 sample but eight at 900. Per-child frequency explains the levels only in a graded way at these sizes.
- Layout is not a small second factor at 12-16 MB: under pinned performance, the sweep with adjacency about 0.94 read 9.57 vs 11.03-11.07 ns at 12 MB (13%), larger than the reviewer's 3.55% effect. The ondemand sw1 children at 4/6 MB had adjacency 0.0 and read highest (6 MB 9.195 vs 8.69-8.90).
- Background activity during G5 that could itself drive the ondemand governor: lmb-frames.py scanning /proc and pagemap every 50 ms on cpu6, nocsample on cpu7, the desktop session and the idle protected VM. None of these is separated out, but all are present in all three states.
- Earlier audit (wf2-results/audit-fast-state.md, read after forming these verdicts) also cites a fully masked protected-mode N90 run (try1, raw files absent) that switched from slow to fast at iteration 4. Together with the VHE iteration-3/8 episodes I reproduced, this means 'quiesced = traffic-following, non-quiesced = fast' needs an additional, unrecorded state change on the N90.
