## Additional findings

- What the p-values in this bundle can mean. Each configuration is one boot session per campaign, so every iteration-level p-value and interval compares one session with another. Between two sessions that both lack host stage-2 the same tests give p = 7e-12 (random access 24 MB, vhe minus nvhe, +3.0 ns on the main mode), 2e-8 (16 MB, +1.1 ns), 2e-9 (nvhe standalone minus nvhe CONFIG.host at 16 MB, +1.4 ns) and 5e-8 (sequential 64 MB stride 1024, vhe minus kvmoff, +0.57 ns) (v98_nullpairs.py). The case for a mode effect in random access therefore rests on size and replication, not on those p-values: 6.2 to 8.4 ns against a largest difference between no-stage-2 sessions of 3.2 ns at 24 MB, 1.8 ns at 32 MB, 1.1 ns at 16 MB and at most 0.6 ns at 4 to 12 MB and 48 to 64 MB, seen in two pkvm boot sessions.
- Session selection. Ten host boot sessions are known: eight in the normal state, plus nvhe-noisy (in the repository) and pkvm try1 (documented only), both in the fast state. The session-level significance in host-stats#16 and #25 is conditional on leaving those two out. The spread of the platform between sessions (about 35 ns at 64 MB) is five times the pkvm increment, so the increment is measurable only between sessions that are in the same state.
- The host fast state and the pVM guest series share a signature, including at the LLC boundary. From the raw guest reports (used only for this comparison): onset at 3 MB, -26 to -31 % at 3 to 6 MB, about -20 % from 12 to 64 MB, a gap of 35 to 41 ns, correlation 0.96 with the host nvhe-noisy profile. In both fast series the LLC-boundary metric (stride 256, 8 MB) takes its low value in 10 of 10 iterations (pVM guest 9.87-10.15 ns; nvhe-noisy host 10.10-10.81 ns), against 44 low values in 110 iterations of all other host and guest series; the documented try1 median at that point is 10.01 ns (docs/n90-kvm-host/pkvm.md:212). The two values of the LLC-boundary metric and the random-access fast state therefore look like one uncontrolled state. None of the claims in this bundle examines that link.
- Sporadic fast iterations in the clean sessions are confined to large working sets: 1 of 160 cells at 3 to 8 MB against 36 of 240 at 12 to 64 MB in CONFIG.host (Fisher p = 7e-8), 1 of 80 against 15 of 120 in standalone; most are only partly fast. The 'from 3 MB upward' profile is seen only where a whole series is in the state (nvhe-noisy host, pVM guest, documented try1).
- Guest-side observation outside this bundle: the paper's sentence on Figure 8 (scratchpad/paper/paper.txt:229) describes a negative overhead of about 10-20 % above 10 MB for the KylinX modes. In the raw guest reports only the pVM series is lower, from 3 MB, by 26 to 32 % at 3 to 6 MB and 17 to 22 % from 8 MB by means; the NP VM series stays within 3.6 % of the ordinary VMs.
- 'Fixed cost per walk' is not what the counts give. Combining the N90 increment with the walks per access measured on the N80 (results/memlat-n80-20260930/expA.log, which I reproduced) gives 3.6 to 4.0 ns per walk at 2 to 4 MB and 6.5 to 8.1 ns from 8 MB upward. The increment correlates almost as well with L3 refills per access (0.95) as with walks per access (0.98).
- The LLC-boundary interval ([-0.47, +1.22] ns) contains zero, but it also contains what the random-access cost per walk would give at stride 256 (7.05 ns / 16 = 0.44 ns = +3.9 %, numerically close to the observed +0.40 ns). What argues against a walk cost of that size in sequential access is the 64 MB stride-256 point (upper limit +0.22 ns in CONFIG.host, +0.15 ns by medians in standalone), not the 8 MB point itself.
- Provenance gaps. The standalone logs have no timestamp, kernel version or command line; scripts/standalone-mem-bench.sh and the try1 raw files are in no git ref of the lmbench repository (git rev-list --all --objects). Statements about the standalone procedure, about its boot sessions and about try1 are documentary.
- Unrecorded host state. prepare-host.sh:41-44 and scripts/verify-clean-env.sh:50 set and check only the top-level transparent-hugepage switch, while the measured kernel (klinux tag 6.6.0-73.76, mm/huge_memory.c:80-83 and 753-754) has per-size anonymous hugepage switches; by default only the 2 MiB size follows the top-level switch and the others are off. Neither these switches nor any devfreq state were recorded in June 2026. This is a recording gap, not evidence of a cause.
