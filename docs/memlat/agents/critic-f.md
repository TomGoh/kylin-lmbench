## Unverified gaps

- N90 buffer layout per lmbench child (E2 on the N90): no frame numbers were recorded in June; every E2 statement about the N90 is carried over from the N80.
- E1 (interconnect frequency or other platform state): no reading other than 1800000 exists anywhere under results/, on any machine; the N90's devfreq governor and limits in June were never recorded.
- THP setting of the June N90 runs: it rests only on the script default (prepare-host-v10-mmap.sh) and a summary line; the summary files carry no THP field. The non-quiesced run's THP and governor are also unknown.
- N90 allocator state and tunables (pcp high/count, pcp_batch_override, kymm_pool/enabled, percpu_pagelist_high_fraction, min_free_kbytes, watermark_scale_factor) were never read; the N90 pcp floor (about 1400-1500 / 2100-2200 pages) is computed only.
- N80 per-CPU decay timeline during H7 (count falls from about 17k to the floor before the 6/8 MB children) is inferred from source plus timestamps; there are no intermediate readings. H7's four sweeps also alternate hold 0 / 2560 MB, which is confounded with sweep order and starting pcp state.
- Whether H7 sweep 1's unbroken 6->8 MB hand-back means 'no drain' or 'a drain of unpaired frames': undecidable from the data. The 6 MB child had only 2 paired frames of 1537 (my h7pair.py).
- N80 host stage-2 block sizes (the '1 GiB-candidate' and '2 MiB' slot labels) were never read from the EL2 tables; they are inferred from source, the memory map and VM frame lists.
- Which hypervisor implementation ran on the N90 in June is unchecked: the 6.6.0-73.76 defconfig has no CONFIG_X1_RMS=y line, while 6.6.0-79.93's kylinos_generic_defconfig sets it. The N90 June build config was not consulted, so N80 (Rust xhypervisor) to N90 (possibly C nvhe) transfer of stage-2 behaviour is unverified.
- D80F3 workstation as a 'no stage-2' baseline: same MIDR, but an IPA limit of 48 bits against 40, kernel 7.0, load average 5-7 and the ondemand governor; walker and firmware equivalence cannot be shown, and walks per hop there are unknown (no PMU).

- N90 walk rates per access in random access were never measured (N80 Exp A rates are used); the N90 per-walk cost at 2-4 MB (about 6-7 cycles) does not fit a fixed per-walk cost.
- Exp X1/X2/X3, Y, Z, V3 and T2 were not recomputed by the recompute audit; values quoted from them (for example the Exp Z class costs) rest on one audit only.

## Contradictions between verifiers

- nh#3, primary against adversary. The primary says E2 is not shown to be unable to produce the isolated 8 MB point: an all-or-nothing hand-back that succeeds about 20% of the time would explain both isolated and carried highs, and the N80's 6->8 MB failures (3/4) make an unmeasured N90 failure 'not implausible'. The adversary says the frame-order E2 cannot produce it unless the hand-back failed almost completely in about 4 of 5 sweeps, which the code makes unlikely. Resolution from primary sources: the N80 failures were partial (24%, 21%, 63% returned) and hit PAIRED frames (sweep 2: 1082/1082 paired lost against 88/455 unpaired; my h7pair.py, matching the kernel-alloc audit). Partial failures would give intermediate 12 MB values, but the N90 has only 7 values in 1.10-1.22, and the isolated highs show no mean elevation (review-layout: -0.005 to +0.015). So the N80 failures do not make an N90 failure of scattered, unpaired frames plausible. I side with the adversary's direction ('disfavoured'), worded as not excluded, because N90 frames and pairing are unmeasured.
- nh#4, primary ('the evidence leaves open') against adversary ('disfavours frame-order E2, compatible with E1'). Both reject a global 'E1 more likely'. The review-layout audit's split reconciles them, and I adopt it: isolated highs (72/93) disfavour frame-order E2; carried highs (21/93, concentrated in VHE) fit both. Both verifiers and the audit agree that cross-stride independence does not discriminate, and that the random-access dips are not shown to be the same state as the sequential levels.
- nh#4 count of high values at 8 MB, stride 256: the primary says 'kvmoff 4, pkvm 5 plus one intermediate'; the adversary says '4 against 6 (one intermediate)'. These describe the same data. My n90chk.py gives kvmoff 12.205/12.242/12.37/12.486 plus 10.98, and pkvm 12.474-12.57 (5) plus 11.772. Report as 4 (+1 at 10.98) against 5 (+1 at 11.77).
- nh#2 group bounds: the claim says 0.94-1.10 and 1.22-1.30. Both verifiers say the lower group starts at 0.962. The review-layout audit gives the upper group as 1.15-1.284 by including the four 1.15-1.207 values. I recomputed: 71 values at 0.962-1.081, 7 at 1.100-1.207, 15 at 1.236-1.284.
- H7 sweep 1 as evidence: the nn-alloc adversary treats it as a counter-example to the worklog-11.30 drain mechanism (the 6->8 MB hand-back survived although high started at 1781). The kernel-alloc audit says that sweep's 6 MB child had virtually no paired frames, so a drain would leave no trace. I checked: 2 paired frames of 1537. The kernel-alloc reading holds, and sweep 1 is not decisive either way.
- nh#3 kernel basis: the nh adversary and primary rely on 'the pcp code is functionally the same at both tags'. The kernel-alloc audit agrees the code is identical, but says identity does not make the hand-back an invariant. The review-layout audit notes that the zap path in mm/memory.c was refactored between the tags and was not traced. These are not contradictory, but 'same code, therefore same behaviour' must not be used.
- nn#17(b) and nn#14: the earlier nn-layout verifiers confirmed nn#14's per-stride ratios. The nn-alloc adversary and review-layout audit show that the match is not E2- or stage-2-specific: about 1.2 in all four N90 boots, against 1.06-1.11 on the stage-2-free D80F3 at strides 64-256. The numbers stand; their use as E2 support is the disagreement, and I resolve against that use.

## Missing for root cause

- LLC boundary +3.55% (N90 host, Figure 7, 8 MB stride 256): well supported now is that the figure is a difference of means between bimodal samples. kvm-off has 4 high values (+1 intermediate), pkvm 5 (+1 intermediate), Fisher p = 0.66. VHE, which has no stage-2, is even higher (+6.8%), and within each level the boots differ by about 0.5-1.6%. What is still missing is the cause of the bimodality itself; without it the reply can say 'not a protected-mode overhead' but not 'the root cause is X'.
- LLC boundary, needed 1: a repeated N90 measurement with at least 2 interleaved boots per mode and more iterations, reporting the distribution or median of the 8 MB point. This would show the high-level frequency is mode-independent.
- LLC boundary, needed 2: an E1-versus-E2 discriminator on the N90 itself. One option is per-child frame numbers (run30 / lmb-frames.py) recorded together with interconnect/devfreq sampling every 100 ms during sweeps, alternating instrumented and uninstrumented sweeps. Another is a private lat_mem_rd that prints every repetition time per child: mixed fast and slow repetitions within a child mean a platform state; a constant level per child that varies between children means layout. A third is a fixed-buffer test that times one 8 MB buffer for minutes.
- LLC boundary, needed 3: layoutshim sorted against shuffled8 on the N90 itself, in kvm-off and pkvm boots (needs THP madvise, a settings change for the user). If sorted buffers still show two levels, E2 is out. The run also measures the N90's own layout ratio per stride and whether it depends on stage-2.
- LLC boundary, needed 4: a mechanism that explains the stride dependence (0/40 at strides 16 and 32, 4/40 at 64, 17-28/40 at 128-1024) and why the high level is about 19% above the N90's own DRAM-bound 12-64 MB plateau and confined to the 8 MB (L3-sized) point. Neither E1 nor E2 has reproduced this on any machine.
- LLC boundary, needed 5: read-only records of the N90 environment in the June configuration: devfreq governors and limits, THP, the pcp tunables and the kymm pool state.
- 64 MB random +3.25% (N90 host, Figure 7, lat_mem_rd -t): the explanation, protected-mode host page walks through the host stage-2, is a well-supported hypothesis. The N90 increment exists only in the pkvm boot (6.4-8.1 ns at 8-64 MB against the pooled median of the other boots; nVHE minus kvm-off is within 0.24 ns) and starts where TLB misses start. On the N80 the walk cost depends on the stage-2 class of the data page within one boot. The paper's 3.25% is a mean that fast-state iterations distort; the medians give +3.4-3.6% (my n90chk.py: pkvm/kvm-off median 1.0351, mean 1.0325).
- 64 MB random, needed 1: a same-machine boot-mode comparison with PMU, on the N90 (or the N80), at least 2 boots per mode: walks per access, cycles per walk, lmbench -t and probe3/frames2 tlb. Prediction: in non-protected boots there is no slot dependence and the lmbench increment disappears.
- 64 MB random, needed 2: a 2x2 of protected/non-protected against 4 KiB/2 MiB data pages. Hugepage-backed buffers should remove most of the protected-mode increment if the cost is in the stage-2 part of the walk. This would also exclude an LLC- or DRAM-side component, which the N90 L3-refill correlation (0.95) cannot.
- 64 MB random, needed 3: direct evidence of the host stage-2 leaf sizes (an EL2 table dump or a kernel that reports them). The class labels and the per-walk cost prediction for the N90 (at least about 15-16 cycles against the observed 11.6-14.6) depend on them, as does the unexplained 2-4 MB shortfall (about 6-7 cycles per walk).
- 64 MB random, needed 4: confirmation of which hypervisor (C nvhe or Rust xhypervisor) and which kernel config ran on the N90 in June, since the N80 evidence comes from 6.6.0-79.93 with CONFIG_X1_RMS=y.
- 64 MB random, needed 5: more than one protected-mode boot on the N90 (pkvm is a single boot, try2) and an explicit treatment of the fast-state iterations, so that the per-access increment rather than a mean ratio is what the reply cites.
- Both numbers: keep the data classes apart in the reply. The figures and boot-mode comparisons are N90 host data (Figure 7). The mechanism evidence comes from the N80 device (protected mode only, background pVM, THP always) and the D80F3 workstation (no stage-2, loaded). Figure 8 guest data are not addressed by any of this.
