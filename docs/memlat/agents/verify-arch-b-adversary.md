Statement that holds: Applying Kessler and Hill's binomial page-placement model (their Eq. 1-2) to the given L3 geometry (128 page colours, 16 pages per colour): with independent, uniformly random placement the expected number of pages in excess of the 16 ways of their colour (Kessler and Hill's 'conflicts'), as a share of the footprint, is 0.08% at 4 MiB, 2.0% at 6 MiB, 9.9% at 8 MiB (95% range over placements 8.6-11.2%), 15.8% at 9 MiB of which 11.1% is unavoidable, and 33.6% at 12 MiB of which 33.3% is unavoidable. The share of pages that sit in an over-subscribed colour is a different and much larger quantity (0.8%, 15%, 53%, 71%, 97%). In the model, unnecessary conflicts peak exactly when the footprint equals the cache size. For stride 256 at 8 MB the 32768 touched lines equal the capacity of the 2048 sets they map to. This locates a sensitivity of the model at 8 MiB. It does not show that colour conflicts cause the 8 MB instability: the model predicts a narrow, single-peaked spread, and on the N80 (protected mode only) real placements reproduce the model's conflict count while latency is unrelated to it and follows physical contiguity.

## Additional findings

- Not resolvable is not the same as zero. At stride 256 the N80 log (expB.log, protected mode) shows 0.053 second-level TLB refills per hop at 8 MB. Multiplied by the +7.1 ns random-access offset this gives about +0.38 ns (+3.4%), almost exactly the observed +0.397 ns (+3.55%). The 8 MB data (95% interval -5.1% to +12.2%) can neither confirm nor exclude this. At 64 MB the same arithmetic predicts +0.44 ns (+4.2%), which the data do exclude (pkvm-kvmoff +0.03 ns, interval -1.7% to +2.3%; standalone +0.12 ns), so in sequential access most of the walk cost is hidden. A reply to the reviewer should say 'cannot be resolved', not 'no effect'.
- The 3.55% does not replicate. In results/standalone-mem-bench (a second boot per mode, 10 runs each) stride 256 at 8 MB gives pkvm-kvmoff +0.65% (p=0.89), -2.81% against nvhe and -3.01% against vhe. The investigator's evidence for claims 13 and 14 did not use these logs.
- The instability belongs to the sequential measurement, not to the 8 MB footprint. Random access at the same 8 MB has CV 0.18-0.51% and resolves pkvm +7.05 ns (+6.26%) with complete separation. The sequential series stays noisy (CV 2-10%) at every size from 8 to 64 MB, against at most 0.26% at 1-4 MB.
- A mode-independent platform state acts on DRAM-bound latency in the N90 data. Random-access iterations show sporadic fast excursions of 5-21% in all four modes (kvmoff 16 MB iterations 2, 7, 8: 132.4, 134.9, 142.8 against 168.5 ns; pkvm 64 MB iteration 6: 166.4 against about 192 ns). The non-quiesced nVHE session has all ten 8 MB iterations in the fast state (mean 10.34 ns, CV 1.8%) yet is 7-12% slower at 64 MB. This is consistent with, but does not prove, the interconnect frequency-scaling hypothesis. The paper's mean-based +3.25% at 64 MB random is affected by these excursions (medians give +3.5%).
- Session confounds visible in the raw report headers: each mode is a single boot, and the pkvm session ran at midday (4 June 12:05-13:37) while the three baselines ran at night (3 June 22:20 to 4 June 03:34). The pkvm file is a second attempt ('try2'). docs/n90-kvm-host/standalone-memory-bench-validation.md:18-45 reports that the first attempt dropped 17-33% in random latency from iteration 4 on; its raw files are neither in the tree nor in git history, so this cannot be checked.

The N80 survey shows no DDR memory unit devfreq device at all, only the network-on-chip device (600-1800 MHz, 54 recorded transitions), whose governor the desktop power manager switches between simple_ondemand, powersave and performance. The state of this device during the June N90 runs was not recorded.
