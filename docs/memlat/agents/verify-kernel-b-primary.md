## kernel-mechanism#18: partly-correct (confidence medium)

Claim under test: Root-cause reading of the two reviewer numbers. The 64 MB random +3.25% is the stage-2 cost: with 4 KiB stage-1 pages the TLB entry size and therefore the number of TLB misses are the same in all four modes, but under pKVM every miss needs a nested walk; the overhead is zero while the working set fits the TLB (up to about 2 MB), ramps between 768 and 2048 pages (second-level TLB reach of the order of 1000 to 2000 entries) and then stays at a constant 6.5 to 8 ns per load, which is what a fixed extra walk cost of one to three cached descriptor reads per miss looks like; a software trap would cost hundreds of ns, not 13 cycles. The 8 MB sequential +3.55% is not a stage-2 effect: a stride-256 walk misses the TLB once per 16 loads, so stage-2 can add at most 7.05/16 = 0.44 ns (3.9%) if fully exposed, the 12 to 64 MB sequential rows show only about +0.1 ns on average, and at 8 MB two baselines with identical translation regimes differ by twice the pkvm effect.

Statement that holds: (HOST data) Inference, partly supported. Supported: the pkvm extra in random access is absent for 0.75 to 2 MB (-0.12 to +0.15 ns), appears at 3 MB and stays at 6.5 to 8.1 ns per load from 8 to 64 MB (11.7 to 14.6 cycles at the real 1.80 GHz clock); protected is the only mode whose host runs under stage-2, host RAM is pre-mapped so no EL2 entry is involved, and the size profile follows the TLB-miss rate measured on the N80. Not shown by any N90 measurement: that the number of TLB misses is equal in the four modes (no counters were recorded on the N90; N80 counters exist only in protected mode) and what the extra consists of. The extra per TLB miss is not constant: about 3.8 ns at 3-4 MB and 6.5 to 8 ns from 8 MB when normalised with the N80 miss rates; and +0.36/+0.63 ns appear at 0.375/0.5 MB. For the 8 MB sequential row the supportable wording is 'cannot be attributed to stage-2', not 'is not a stage-2 effect': the bound 7.05/16 = 0.44 ns (3.9% of 11.2 ns) is larger than the observed +0.40 ns (+3.55%), so the magnitude alone does not exclude it. What prevents attribution is that the difference is not significant (p = 0.40, bimodal data), that two stage-1-only boots differ by +6.77%, that the 12-64 MB sequential rows show +0.10 ns on average, and that a second set of runs gives +0.65%. The trap-cost argument is reasoning, not a measurement in these data.
