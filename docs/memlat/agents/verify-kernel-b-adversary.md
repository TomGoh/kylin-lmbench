## kernel-mechanism#18: partly-correct (confidence medium)

Claim under test: Root-cause reading of the two reviewer numbers. The 64 MB random +3.25% is the stage-2 cost: with 4 KiB stage-1 pages the TLB entry size and therefore the number of TLB misses are the same in all four modes, but under pKVM every miss needs a nested walk; the overhead is zero while the working set fits the TLB (up to about 2 MB), ramps between 768 and 2048 pages (second-level TLB reach of the order of 1000 to 2000 entries) and then stays at a constant 6.5 to 8 ns per load, which is what a fixed extra walk cost of one to three cached descriptor reads per miss looks like; a software trap would cost hundreds of ns, not 13 cycles. The 8 MB sequential +3.55% is not a stage-2 effect: a stride-256 walk misses the TLB once per 16 loads, so stage-2 can add at most 7.05/16 = 0.44 ns (3.9%) if fully exposed, the 12 to 64 MB sequential rows show only about +0.1 ns on average, and at 8 MB two baselines with identical translation regimes differ by twice the pkvm effect.

Statement that holds: [H for the timings, D for the counts] The evidence supports reading the host-side 64 MB random offset as the cost of stage-2 during page walks: the pkvm offset is about zero where no second-level TLB refills occur (0.75 to 2 MB), grows from 3 to 8 MB and stays at 6.5 to 8.1 ns (12 to 15 cycles) from 8 MB on, following the l2d_tlb_refill rate measured on the N80 in protected mode (0.05 / 0.19 / 0.45 / 0.84 / 0.97 / 1.00 per load at 2 / 3 / 4 / 6 / 8 / 16+ MB, i.e. a second-level TLB of roughly 1000 to 1300 entries), and a separate standalone run set reproduces it. The clean size at 64 MB is +6.5 to +6.9 ns (+3.5 to +3.7% by medians); the paper's +3.25% is the mean-based value. Left open: that the number of TLB misses is equal in the four modes on the N90 (no counters were taken there; the N80 counts are protected-only), that the extra cost per miss is fixed (offset per refill is about 3.8 ns at 3-4 MB against 6.3 to 8.1 ns from 6 MB), and the 'one to three descriptor reads' decomposition. The 8 MB sequential +3.55% is not attributable to stage-2, but the 0.44 ns bound does not show that (0.44 ns is 3.9%, more than 3.55%); what shows it is that the row is bimodal in every mode, pkvm equals the baselines within each level, the standalone set gives +0.65% against kvmoff and a negative sign against nvhe and vhe, and the sign flips with stride. A stage-2 contribution of about 0.1 ns (the 12 to 64 MB average) cannot be excluded.

## kernel-mechanism#22: partly-correct (confidence high)

- The name 'LLC boundary (about 8 MB)' does not match the hardware behaviour: on the N80 the L3 refill rate per load in the random test is already 0.41 at 3 MB and 0.62 at 4 MB (0.06 at 2 MB), with no page-colour overflow recorded (color_max 9 of 16). The jump of the baseline from 20.7 ns at 2 MB to 36.6 ns at 3 MB on the N90 host is therefore a mix of L3 misses and TLB misses, not a TLB effect alone.
- The 8 MB sequential row is bimodal in every quiesced host mode (about 10.2-10.6 ns or about 12.3-12.4 ns per iteration) and its mean only reflects how many of the 10 iterations landed high. Figure 7 itself shows the sign problem: the same bar is +3.55% against kvmoff, +2.87% against nVHE and -3.02% against VHE. In the non-quiesced nVHE run the row is not bimodal at all (0 of 10 high, 10.1-10.8 ns).
- The internal docs that say the kvm-off kernel runs at EL1 (docs/n90-kvm-host/kvmoff.md section 1.3) and that 'kvm-arm.mode=vhe' is parsed as a mode (vhe.md note at line 61) are wrong according to the source at the measurement tag: 'vhe' is rejected with -EINVAL and both configurations boot the kernel at EL2 with VHE. I checked this because claim 17 depends on it.
- The earliest N90 data (2026-06-01/02, results/n90-day1 and n90-day2) were taken on a different image, 6.6.0-52-generic #43pkvm10-KYLINOS built 2026-04-13, with a different root file system UUID. That build is not a tag of klad-v11-stable, so its EL2 implementation cannot be determined from the tree; these data are not the Figure 7 data but they should not be mixed with them.
