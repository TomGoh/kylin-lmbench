## nh#3: partly-correct (confidence medium)

Claim under test: If the high 8 MB level on the N90 came from scattered frames (E2), and the 12 MB child took those frames back as every 12 MB child did on the N80 (Exp H7: 2033-2034 of the 8 MB child's 2049 frames in 4 of 4 sweeps; the same code at both kernel tags), then by Exp H8 the 12 MB point would read about 1.16-1.19 times the low level after nearly every high 8 MB point. The N90 shows that in about one fifth of the cases and shows almost no intermediate values. So E2 cannot produce the isolated high 8 MB point unless the N90's allocator failed to hand the frames back in about four of five sweeps; and the hand-back is what E2 uses to explain the carry-over to the next point.

Statement that holds: If the high 8 MB level on the N90 came from the ORDER of scattered frames (the effect Exp H8 measured on the N80 with frame sets taken from whole 2 MiB blocks), and the 12 MB child took those frames back as every 12 MB child did on the N80 (Exp H7, nothp, stride 256 only: 2033-2034 of the 8 MB child's 2049 frames in 4 of 4 sweeps, handed back in exactly reversed virtual order; the pcp code is functionally the same at both kernel tags), then, if the N80 effect sizes carry over to the N90, the 12 MB point would read about 1.16-1.19 times the low level (H8 strides 128/256 only) after nearly every high 8 MB point. The N90 shows a 12 MB value above 1.12 after 19 of 93 high 8 MB points, with few intermediate values. So the frame-order form of E2 cannot produce the isolated high 8 MB point unless the N90 hand-back failed almost completely in about four of five sweeps. The code makes such a failure unlikely, but it was never observed on the N90 because no frames and no pcp state were recorded there. Not excluded by H8: effects of WHICH frames a buffer gets (page colour / L3 set or DRAM bank/channel distribution), and a different size of the order effect on the N90.

Evidence: Frame hand-back: own script h7.py over expH7-pfns.log (132 children, split into 4 sweeps by the '## rep=' start times in expH7.log). 8 MB (2049 pages) to 12 MB (3073 pages): common frames 2034/2033/2034/2033. The shared frames sit at positions 15/16 to 2048 of the 12 MB child, and 2033-2034 of them satisfy B[i] == A[-1-i], so the hand-back is LIFO and reverses the order. The 8 MB children had ascending adjacency 0.000/0.000/0.003/0.192. Kernel: git diff 6.6.0-73.76 6.6.0-79.93 -- mm/page_alloc.c changes only UP-safe pcp locking, page->private=0, removal of the free_count init and page_frag_cache_drain; nr_pcp_high, free_unref_page_commit, decay_pcp_high and zone_highsize are identical (73.76 lines 2322-2350, 2507-2625, 5596-5730). By the code, on a 32 GB machine high_max = managed/8/split_cpus is larger than on the N80. Freeing more than pcp->high pages bulk-frees the oldest list entries (list tail), which keeps the most recently freed pages. A failure needs pcp->high below about 2100 at the 8 MB child's exit, or ZONE_BELOW_HIGH/RECLAIM_ACTIVE. Even a 50% failure leaves a scattered share of about 0.5, so H8 predicts about 1.12, not the ~1.0 seen in 58 of 93. Order alternatives tested: (a) a contiguous 8 MB child handed back reversed. expL2-n80.log (thplayout) shows reversed only +3.4% at 8 MB (8.213 vs 7.944 ns) and +7% at 16 MB (8.666 vs 8.068), so it cannot create or hide a 20% level. (b) A scattered child stays scattered when reversed. Set/colour alternative: H8's shuffled8 uses the same frames as sorted (four whole 2 MiB blocks, perfectly even colours), so it says nothing about frame-set effects. Against a pure L3-colour variant: on the N90 the low 8 MB level already equals the DRAM level (stride 256: 10.37 vs 64 MB median 10.44; stride 128: about 9.3 vs 9.12), and the high level (12.35) is about 19% above it, more than losing the L3 could cost. So the colour variant is implausible but not excluded, and a bank/channel-set variant is untested. H8 covers only strides 128/256, while the N90 count spans 64-1024.

## nh#4: partly-correct (confidence medium)

Claim under test: Judgement on the two levels of the N90's 8 MB sequential point (LLC boundary): more likely a platform state that changes from one lmbench child to the next (E1) than the physical layout of the buffer (E2). Supporting E1: the isolated 8 MB point (nh#2, nh#3); the level at one stride is independent of the level at the previous stride of the same run, whereas on the N80 a scattered state persisted over all 32 sweeps; the quiesced N90 boots' own random-access data show 35 values at 12-64 MB in 22 of 40 iterations 3-21% below the usual level, the lowest at the level of the non-quiesced 'fast state', with a ratio of the two states (1.23-1.27) like that of the two sequential levels, and frame order cannot cause this in random access (Exp H6). Not established: E1 has never been measured directly (no interconnect frequency other than 1800 MHz on any machine; nothing recorded on the N90). E2 remains a real, separately demonstrated sensitivity of this measurement point (19% at 8 MB with lmbench's own program). In either case the +3.55% is the difference in how many of ten iterations fell on the high level, not an overhead of protected mode.

Statement that holds: Judgement on the two levels of the N90's 8 MB sequential point: the available data disfavour the frame-order form of E2 with LIFO hand-back (isolated 8 MB point, nh#2/nh#3) and are compatible with a state that changes from one lmbench child to the next (E1). They do not establish E1. E1 would have to be size- and timing-specific, because the high state appears in 93 of 200 sweeps at 8 MB but only 22/200 at 12 MB and 5/200 at 64 MB, so a generic platform state that hits every child equally is contradicted. One candidate is a traffic-driven interconnect governor that ramps up during the first DRAM-heavy child; it has never been measured, and nothing about the interconnect was recorded on the N90. The level at one stride is independent of the level at the previous stride of the same run (P(high | previous high) = 0.52, P(high | previous low) = 0.58), but this rules out only a persistent layout state, not a per-child random layout. The quiesced boots' random-access data contain 35 values at 12-64 MB in 22 of 40 iterations that are 4-21% below the boot's usual level, the lowest at the non-quiesced 'fast state' level, which is 1.23-1.27x faster at 12-64 MB. However, the quiesced fast values never occur at 3-8 MB (0 of 40 at each size), where the non-quiesced state is 1.34-1.55x faster, so it is not shown that they are the same state. Frame order cannot cause the random-access effect (Exp H6), and THP backing does not fit the non-quiesced profile. E2 remains a real, separately demonstrated sensitivity of this point on the N80 (about +20-21% at 8 MB with lmbench's own program, strides 128/256). In either case the +3.55% is the difference between 4 and 6 of ten iterations on the high level (levels about 10.4 and 12.35 ns in both boots); the data show no protected-mode overhead at this point, and 4/10 vs 6/10 is within chance.

## Additional findings

- nh#1: the linear scattered-share rule holds only up to 24 MB. At stride 256 from 32 MB up, head8 stays above it (medians 1.100/1.079/1.068 at 32/48/64 MB vs predicted 1.084/1.057/1.044, i.e. +1.5% to +2.3%); at stride 128 it stays within 1%. This does not affect the 12 MB argument in nh#3 but the general phrase 'follows linearly' should keep the 12-24 MB qualifier.
- nh#2: the 93 high-8 MB sweeps split 57/16/19 plus one sweep whose 12 MB value is below 0.97 (0.962); the claim's three bins sum to 92. The lower 12 MB group spans 0.962-1.105, so '0.94-1.10' should read '0.96-1.10'.
- Hand-back is LIFO: the 12 MB child receives the 8 MB child's frames in exactly reversed virtual order (B[i] == A[-1-i] for all 2033-2034 shared frames in expH7-pfns.log). Any E2 argument that treats the 12 MB child's inherited layout as 'the same layout' should say 'the same frames, reversed'. Per expL2-n80.log, reversal of a contiguous buffer costs only +3% at 8 MB and +7% at 16 MB.
- Size specificity cuts against generic E1 as much as against E2. The high state is concentrated at the LLC-size point (8 MB 93/200 vs 12 MB 22, 64 MB 5). The N90's low 8 MB level equals its 64 MB (DRAM) level, and the high level is about 19% above DRAM level. Whatever the mechanism, it must make the 8 MB child, and almost only that child, slower than a fully DRAM-bound buffer. Neither E1 nor E2 has reproduced this on any machine.
- Strides 16 and 32 are rightly excluded from the two-level count: their 8 MB values are flat (spread within 1%, apart from one 2.85 among 2.63-2.67 at stride 32). Stride 64 is high in only 4 of 40, against 17-28 of 40 at strides 128-1024.
- Kernel tags: the 6.6.0-73.76 to 6.6.0-79.93 diff of mm/page_alloc.c leaves the pcp auto-tuning logic (nr_pcp_high, nr_pcp_free, free_unref_page_commit, decay_pcp_high, zone_highsize, zone_set_pageset_high_and_batch) unchanged; mm_init.c differs only in a __meminitdata annotation.
- No earlier wf2 report covers the nh# claims. audit-fast-state.md independently reached the same conclusion as my THP check: THP does not match the N90 fast-state profile at 0.5-2 MB.
