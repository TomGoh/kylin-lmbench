## Additional findings

- Carry-over of the LLC-boundary high state to the next measurement (not covered by any claim, and it contradicts the 'decided per measurement' framing of host-stats#10): in the four quiesced CONFIG.host sessions, over strides 128-1024, 20 of the 21 slow 12 MB sequential cells (more than 1 ns above their configuration x stride median) come directly after an 8 MB cell in the high state; P(slow 12 MB | 8 MB high) = 20/95 against 1/65 after a low 8 MB cell (Fisher p = 0.0002; stratified permutation p = 0.0006); the link extends weakly to the 16 MB cell (15 of 16 at a +0.5 ns cut, p = 0.014) and is absent for the 12 MB cell of a different stride in the same iteration (36 of 63, p = 0.86). The three pkvm and two vhe iterations at 13.1-13.8 ns that produce the 12 MB '+7.64 %' in host-stats#8 are all such cells. The state therefore outlives one forked child (or is inherited through reuse of the pages the previous child freed) but does not last until the next stride sweep.
- A session-level state can switch the phenomenon off: the non-quiesced nVHE session (n90-nvhe-noisy-full; its reports show gvfsd-fuse and debug tracefs mounted, i.e. desktop services not masked) has 0 of 40 high cells at 8 MB over strides 128-1024 against 95 of 160 in the four quiesced sessions (Fisher p = 1e-13) and no slow 12 MB cell. Together with the fast random-access state of that session this points to an uncontrolled platform condition that differs between quiesced and non-quiesced boots; the host data do not record what it is.
- Sequential access at 12-64 MB is not neutral between the retained sessions: averaged over those sizes pkvm (try2) is above kvmoff by +2.7 to +4.6 % at strides 64-1024 (permutation p < 0.05 at every stride) and above nvhe by +1.6 to +4.8 %, and below vhe by 2.4 to 5.5 %, while vhe is above kvmoff by 5.2 to 8.8 %. With one boot per configuration this cannot be assigned to the KVM mode, but the paper's 'Main Mem +0.29 %' (stride 256, 64 MB) is the smallest of the stride-256 differences from 12 to 64 MB (+0.3 to +7.6 %) and of the 64 MB differences over strides 64-1024 (+1.2, +1.95, +0.29, +2.73, +3.50 %).
- The saturated random-access increment is neither constant nor identical against the three baselines: against vhe it is only +5.2 ns (24 MB) and +5.9 ns (32 MB) because the vhe session itself is 2-3 ns slower there, and in both campaigns the 64 MB increment (6.2-6.6 ns) is about 0.8 ns below the 8 MB increment (7.1-7.4 ns), with the standalone 32 MB value at 8.3 ns. A model of one fixed extra cost per access from 8 MB upward fits to within about 1 ns, not exactly.
- Provenance gaps that limit what can be verified from primary sources: (a) the standalone logs carry no timestamps, uptime, kernel command line or boot mode; their mode and session identity rest on the file names and on docs/n90-kvm-host/standalone-memory-bench-validation.md, and the run script that document cites (scripts/standalone-mem-bench.sh) is in no commit; (b) the pkvm column is the second run (try2); the first run (try1), documented as switching to a roughly 20 % faster state from iteration 4, was removed from the repository (commit 18e4c3a6), so the choice cannot be audited; (c) two earlier 5-iteration runs (kvmoff, vhe, 2026-06-03) survive only as summary files; (d) the script that draws Figure 7 is not in the repository.
- The N80 device data cannot be transferred directly to the N90 two-valued behaviour: in results/memlat-n80-20260930/expB.log the 8 MB stride-256 latency is continuous (8.25-9.74 ns over 30 fresh processes, largest gap 0.2 ns) and tracks physical contiguity (Spearman -0.86), whereas on the N90 it takes two separated values (about 10.3 and 12.4 ns). Any statement that contiguity explains the N90 bimodality is an extrapolation across machines, kernel versions (6.6.0-79 vs 6.6.0-73), THP settings and system state.
- Estimator details behind three quoted figures: the Spearman coefficients in host-stats#10 are computed after subtracting each configuration's median (other centrings give +0.14 to +0.35 for the 12 MB neighbour); the percent bootstrap interval in host-stats#5 is a bootstrapped ratio of means rather than the ns interval divided by the kvmoff mean ([-4.3 %, +11.1 %]); the '1.04 ns within-configuration sd' in host-stats#6 is the sd of the 30 pooled no-stage-2 values (pooled within-configuration sd 1.03 ns, n = 106 instead of 108). None changes a conclusion.
- Boundary cells are unstable in general, not only at 8 MB: in random access at the L1d boundary (0.047 and 0.0625 MB) the kvmoff medians are 0.8-0.9 ns (22-30 %) below pkvm, nvhe and vhe alike, and at the L2 boundary (0.375-0.5 MB) pkvm is 0.3-1.0 ns above all three baselines, then 0.06-0.31 ns below them at 0.75 MB. Any single cache-boundary cell compared between two boots can show a difference of several percent without a stage-2 cause.
