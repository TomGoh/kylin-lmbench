## Additional findings

- H4 has a layout effect at the L2-cache boundary that neither the claims nor the worklog mention. At 0.25, 0.375 and 0.5 MB the shuffled and natural buffers are 1.6 to 7.9 percent slower than sorted on the N80 (0.5 MB: 3.545 against 3.80-3.83 ns, in all four repetitions), and the workstation shows the same (2.716 against 2.92-2.94 ns). The per-seed pattern is nearly identical on the two machines, which fits a cache-colour effect fixed by the permutation. Worklog line 1339 ('4 MB 及以下相差 0.7%–1.5%') and the sentence 'all predictions held' in nn#13 are wrong for these sizes.
- The match in nn#14 is a match of ratios only. In nanoseconds the N90 low level at 8 MB equals the N80 shuffled level (0.972, 0.967, 0.972, 0.964, 1.000 for strides 64 to 1024) and is 1.16 to 1.20 times the N80 sorted level; the N90 high level is 1.16 to 1.19 times the N80 shuffled level. That factor is plausible (N90 / N80-sorted is 1.18 to 1.24 at 6 MB, 1.055 to 1.06 in cache, 1.19 to 1.20 for random access at 8 to 64 MB), but it is an assumption, not a measurement.
- The ratio match is specific to the 8 MB row, and to 12 MB except stride 1024 (1.172 against 1.259). At 16 to 64 MB and strides 256 to 1024 the few N90 high values are only 1.087 to 1.129 times the low level, where the N80 layout ratio is 1.24 to 1.33.
