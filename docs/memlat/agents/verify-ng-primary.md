## Additional findings

- lat_mem_rd reports the MINIMUM of 11 timed repetitions of at least 0.1 s each (src/lat_mem_rd.c:143-149 save_minimum; src/lib_timing.c:179). Each child spends about 1.1-1.3 s, mostly in timed repetitions, so the 100 ms NoC samples resolve individual repetitions roughly. 'Any 1800 MHz sample during the child' is therefore the right criterion. It explains why low points have only 1-3 high samples in 11-13. The worklog does not state this mechanism.
- Adversarial check of the 12-64 MB ondemand points: the overall ondemand sample tally is dominated by 600 MHz, but that comes from the small sizes and the random sweeps. During the 12-64 MB sequential children the NoC was often at 900-1800 MHz (for example 64 MB: 2-9 samples at 1800 per child, none at 600). At every size from 12 MB up, the children with no 1800 sample are the slowest of their size: 12 MB 13.42 (at the powersave level of 13.43), 16 MB 12.05, 32 MB 11.51, 48 MB 11.12 and 11.37. So these points are consistent with the reading and do not contradict it. At 12 MB, though, two children with three 1800 samples each read 12.07 and 12.33, so sample presence does not classify the points cleanly beyond 8 MB.
- Order effects: the three governors ran in sequence (performance, then ondemand, then powersave) and were not interleaved. Buffer adjacency drifts between the states: at 8 MB it is 0.30-0.92 under performance and 0.07-0.15 under powersave. At 4 and 6 MB, however, adjacency is the same (about 0.91 and 0.65-0.73) in all three states, and ondemand still equals powersave there. Inside ondemand at 8 MB, adjacency does not separate the two levels (Spearman -0.06). The frequency effect therefore survives the order and layout confound. The high 8 MB points cluster in time (sweeps 4-9 and 11 in a row).
- The ng#3 absolute comparisons mix modes. The N80 runs in protected mode, with a host stage-2. The N90 references are kvmoff, nvhe and vhe (quiesced) and the non-quiesced nVHE run. The N90 pkvm 64 MB random median is 191.7 ns. The worklog itself notes about 3% stage-2 overhead, but the claim text leaves that caveat out.
- The N90 non-quiesced 'fast' run is slower than the quiesced runs at sequential 12 MB (11.34 vs 11.09) and 64 MB (11.11 vs 10.46). On the N80 a higher NoC frequency never made those sizes slower. The N90 fast state is therefore not fully explained by NoC frequency; contention from background traffic, or other state, may also play a part.
- On the N80, an unidentified agent replaced the driver default (simple_ondemand) with 'performance' about 16.5 s after boot (README.md:26). The N80 kernel also offers a non-upstream 'stable_ondemand' governor. The N90's runtime governor cannot be inferred from the driver source. It is also unknown whether the =m phytium_noc module was loaded on the N90 in June.
- Minor: the worklog in section 11.34 gives 11.02 as the ondemand 12 MB median, from 12 sweeps, while derived/state-tables.md gives 11.05, from 13 points. The other cells of the same row are not affected by this difference.
