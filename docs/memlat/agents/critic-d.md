- **ek#4**: Refuted by both verifiers, and I confirmed it in the source at tag 6.6.0-79.93: kvm_pgtable_stage2_map always installs a table-post callback (arch/arm64/kvm/hyp/pgtable.c:1204-1239) that runs stage2_coalesce_walk_table_post (1127-1176), which replaces a table whose page count is 1 by a block once kvm_protected_mode_initialized is enabled (arch/arm64/kvm/pkvm.c:481-491). Returning a page to the host goes through host_stage2_idmap_locked into kvm_pgtable_stage2_map with flags 0 (arch/arm64/kvm/hyp/xhypervisor/src/mem_protect/host.rs:314-367 and 766-770). pgtable.c is the same blob (74ee1cfe9de1) at both release tags, so this holds for the June N90 kernel too. Safe wording: By the source at both release tags, the host stage-2 map is merged back into blocks. When a guest or hypervisor page is returned to the host it is mapped again through kvm_pgtable_stage2_map, whose table-post step (stage2_coalesce_walk_table_post, active once protected mode is initialised) replaces a last-level table that holds no other non-host entry by a 2 MiB block, and a level-2 table without remaining tables or annotations by a 1 GiB block. The fragmentation caused by a protected VM therefore lasts while its pages are away, not until reboot. What persists: tables that existed at the end of EL2 initialisation, which are pinned (on the N80 the slots at 0x80000000, 0xc0000000, 0x2080000000 and 0x2340000000), and regions that still hold a hypervisor-owned page, for example heap pages not yet reclaimed. Wording for the paper: 'while a protected VM is running'.

## Unverified gaps

- No claim received an 'unverifiable' verdict. The items below are parts of claims, or premises behind them, that no primary source available in this round can decide.
- Data-kind check. No claim in the usable or softened set uses guest-side (x-kernel) data, and none assigns an N80 number to the N90 host or to Figure 8. None of these transfers is verified; they must stay worded as comparisons. The N80 numbers are host-side measurements taken while a protected VM ran in the background and must not be described as guest-side data.
- N90, Figure 7 runs: that no VM was running. Procedure documents only (docs/n90-kvm-host/pkvm.md:79-90). The raw reports record uname, date, uptime, load, network and mounts, with no process or service list, and scripts/apply-masks.sh was committed after the protected-mode run. (ev#13)

(ev#5, ev#9 to ev#11, ev#14)
- Meaning of the PMU events on the FTC862. ## Missing for root cause

- [64 MB random, +3.25%] A same-machine comparison of boot modes with hardware counters. Every counter measurement (page walks, cycles, cache refills per access) was taken on the N80 in protected mode; every boot-mode difference comes from N90 timing without counters. 'Walk counts are the same in all modes and a walk costs about 11 to 14 cycles more under protected mode' is obtained by dividing N90 time differences by N80 walk counts (my cross-check: 3.7 to 3.9 ns per walk at 3 to 4 MB, 6.2 to 7.9 ns from 6 MB). Needed on one machine and one kernel: protected against nvhe and none, with walks, cycles and refills per access. experiments/memlat-probe/run5-mode-compare.sh is staged and has not been run.
- [64 MB random] A measurement of the quantity itself. The evening experiments compare 4 KiB stage-2 leaves with block mappings inside one protected boot (Exp V, V2). They show, indirectly through L2-cache refills, that a host TLB miss reads stage-2 descriptors. They do not show what a block-mapped stage 2 costs relative to no stage 2, which is what the +3.25% (about +6.5 ns per access by medians) is.
- [64 MB random] Separation of translation cost from memory-path cost. On the N90 the increment appears where page walks appear (nothing up to 2 MB, +0.7 ns at 3 MB, +1.7 ns at 4 MB, +5 to +8 ns from 6 MB), but accesses that leave the core's caches set in over the same sizes. Needed: the boot-mode comparison repeated with the buffer on 2 MiB pages (walks vanish, memory accesses stay), or with a translation-only chase whose data stay in cache.
- [64 MB random] Replication at the level of boots, and a protected run that was not selected. Each mode is one boot of 10 iterations: VHE, kvm-off and nVHE between 2026-06-03 22:20 and 2026-06-04 03:34, protected ('try2') on 2026-06-04 12:05 after a first attempt had been inspected and discarded (its raw files were removed from the repository). The only other collection (results/standalone-mem-bench: protected 192.4 ns against 185.3 to 186.0 ns at 64 MB) has no timestamp, kernel version or command line in its logs. Needed: at least three boots per mode in interleaved order with the state recorded.
- [64 MB random] A defensible figure. +3.25% is a ratio of 10-iteration means (188.13 against 182.21 ns), and each mode's ten values include two or three fast ones. A permutation test gives p about 0.09 on the means and about 0.03 on the medians (+6.5 ns, +3.5%). What needs a cause is the roughly 6.5 ns per access; the published percentage also depends on how many fast iterations each boot happened to contain.
- [64 MB random] The state of the N90 during the Figure 7 runs. The raw reports hold no process or service list, no kernel config, no boot log (IPA size, protected-mode banner), no memory map and no THP or devfreq settings. It is therefore unrecorded whether a VM was running, which EL2 implementation ran, and how the host stage-2 map was shaped (1 GiB blocks, 2 MiB blocks or 4 KiB leaves). If a VM had been running, part of the increment would be the 4 KiB-leaf cost measured on the N80.
- [64 MB random] A direct reading of a host stage-2 table on a measured machine. Everything said about leaf sizes rests on source code and on a user-space run of it; the measured kernels have no dump interface.
- [64 MB random] The dependence on stage-2 block size. The two N80 slots that can still be 1 GiB blocks (0x22c0000000, 0x2300000000) are about 2 ns (3 to 4 cycles) slower per access than the 2 MiB-mapped slots wherever page walks occur (31 of 32 rounds at 12 MB and above in Exp S, V, V2), and the cause is not established. A protected host without a VM, as assumed for Figure 7, would be mapped mostly by 1 GiB blocks, so this bears directly on the number. Needed: stop the VM and re-measure the same slots (the source predicts they return to 1 GiB blocks), or boot protected without a VM and compare complete with incomplete slots, with a non-protected boot as control.
- [64 MB random] Microarchitecture. The TLB, walk caches and prefetchers of the FTC862 are not documented and the PMU events are not validated on this core. How many extra descriptor reads a two-stage walk makes here is an architectural bound, not a measurement, and nothing derives the size of the increment (about 11 to 14 cycles per walk from 6 MB, about 7 at 3 to 4 MB).
- [64 MB random] The object of the paper. The paper describes the Rust hypervisor; the June N90 kernel most likely ran the C implementation, and no record decides it. Needed: the N90's /boot/config-6.6.0-73-generic, or Figure 7 repeated on a kernel with CONFIG_X1_RMS=y.
- [LLC boundary, +3.55%] There is no established protected-mode effect at this point to explain. The figure is the ratio of 10-iteration means at 8 MB, stride 256 (11.600 against 11.203 ns). The point takes two levels, about 10.3 and 12.4 ns, in all four boot modes; protected has 6 of its 10 values on the high level and kvm-off 4; a permutation test gives p about 0.40; against VHE the same figure is -3.0%, and VHE against kvm-off is +6.8% although neither has a host stage 2; the second collection gives +0.65%. A root-cause statement therefore has to explain the two levels, and that explanation is missing.
- [LLC boundary] Identification of what produces the two levels on the N90. Two candidates remain: a platform frequency state (the interconnect devfreq hypothesis) and the physical layout of the benchmark buffer. No N90 measurement records the interconnect governor and frequency, or the frame numbers of the buffer, together with the latency. It is not even recorded whether the interconnect driver was loaded on the N90 in June.
- [LLC boundary] The decisive experiment for the interconnect candidate. No latency has been measured at any interconnect frequency other than 1800 MHz under governor 'performance', on any machine; the governor experiments (run4, run7, run8) are staged and were not run. Needed: the 8 MB point with the governor at simple_ondemand and pinned low, with the frequency logged for every timed interval, preferably on the N90.
- [LLC boundary] Evidence that separates the candidates. The N80 result (0 of 18 high values with lmbench at a pinned interconnect) fits the interconnect candidate but does not exclude layout: the buffer layout was not recorded, the sweeps are not independent, and with the probe the same point moves continuously by 14% to 18% with layout at the same interconnect frequency (8.25 to 9.74 ns). Against layout alone: on the N90 the high level concentrates at 8 MB (28, 22, 17 and 22 of 40 values at strides 128 to 1024, at most 6 of 40 at any other size), whereas the layout effect on the N80 is as large at 16 to 64 MB. Against the interconnect alone: single points of quiesced runs reach the fast level. Records of both variables for every measurement on the N90 would decide.
- [LLC boundary] The mechanism of the layout effect. Virtual order changes sequential latency by 18% to 27% for physically packed pages at a pinned interconnect (Exp W; about 8% to 11% in Exp L), equally for both page classes, so 4 KiB stage-2 leaves are not the cause; descending physical order is as fast as ascending up to 32 MB. Why order matters is unknown, and the effect has been measured only in protected mode.
- [LLC boundary] Statistical power. With 10 iterations per mode and a standard deviation of about 1 ns, the 95% interval of the difference of means is roughly -5% to +12%. About 100 iterations per mode, or a pinned and recorded platform state, would be needed to see or exclude an effect of 3.5%. A walk cost of the size seen in random access, if not overlapped with neighbouring memory accesses, would be about 0.4 ns at this stride (one walk per 16 accesses); the data can neither confirm nor exclude it.
- [LLC boundary] Hardware facts of the N90. The 8 MiB L3 that makes 8 MB the 'LLC boundary' was read on the N80; the N90's cache geometry is not recorded.
- [both] The fast and slow platform states. In the one non-quiesced nVHE run random access from 3 MB is 18% to 35% faster and the 8 MB point is always low. What causes this is not identified: the interconnect hypothesis is untested in the converse direction, and the DDR-controller frequency driver, a desktop session and the pvm-manage service are not excluded. Until it is identified and recorded, differences between boots can be trusted only on medians within one state, and both reviewer figures, being ratios of means, mix in an unknown share of it.
