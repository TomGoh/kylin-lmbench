## ev#12: confirmed (confidence medium)

Claim under test: Interpretation of Exp V and V2: (a) the two classes differ only where the chase needs page walks (no difference at 2 to 4 MB where walks per access are 0; a difference from 6 MB where walks per access are 0.9 or more), (b) the number of walks per access is the same in both classes, so the difference is a cost per walk, and (c) the extra L2-cache refills per access grow with the number of distinct stage-2 leaf descriptors the working set needs: host pages that share a 2 MiB region with pages donated to the protected VM are mapped in the host stage-2 table with 4 KiB leaves, one 8-byte descriptor per page; with the pages spread evenly (about one page in eight of a region) each page's descriptor is on its own cache line, so a 32 MB set needs about 8192 lines (512 KiB, the size of the L2 cache) and nearly every walk misses the L2 cache for it, while a physically contiguous 32 MB set needs 1024 lines (64 KiB) and shows only +0.10 refills. Therefore: in protected mode a host TLB miss reads host stage-2 descriptors, the read costs time, and the cost depends on the stage-2 mapping granularity. This was measured within one boot and one boot mode on the N80 (Rust EL2, kernel 6.6.0-79). It does NOT measure what a block-mapped stage-2 costs relative to no stage-2 at all, which is the quantity behind the paper's +3.25%.

Statement that holds: Holds as an inference; the evidence supports it. Safe wording: on the N80 in protected mode (Rust EL2, kernel 6.6.0-79, one boot), host pages in 2 MiB regions that also hold pages of the running protected VM cost 11 to 36 more user-mode cycles per TLB miss than pages in other regions, with 0.08 to 0.92 more L2-cache refills per access and the same number of page walks; at 2 to 4 MB, where the chase needs no walks, there is no difference. - Unclaimed slot effect that bears on the paper: the two slots with no VM pages and no hypervisor memory (0x22c0000000 and 0x2300000000) are the only ones that, by the kernel source, can still be single 1 GiB stage-2 blocks. They are slower than the seven slots that hold VM pages (0x2100000000 to 0x2280000000, split into 2 MiB blocks) by 1.7 to 2.3 ns, or 3.5 to 4.3 user-mode cycles, per access in every evening experiment (S, V, V2) at 12 MB and above, in all 32 (run, pass) rounds; at 3 and 4 MB, where there are no walks, the difference is -0.2 ns. The slot with hypervisor memory (0x2340000000) and the two low slots, all 2 MiB-mapped, are at the normal level. One reading is that a 1 GiB stage-2 block costs about one more first-level-cache read per walk than a 2 MiB block on this core; a physical difference of those two slots is not excluded, because walks and DRAM accesses set in at the same sizes. This matters because, with no VM running, the N90 host of Figure 7 is mapped almost entirely with 1 GiB blocks (the same prepopulate and split code is in tag 6.6.0-73.76: nvhe/mem_protect.c:196, nvhe/rust/src/mem_protect/host.rs:375 and 546). A direct test: stop the VM and rerun Exp S; the coalescing code (pgtable.c:1127-1176) returns the VM's slots to 1 GiB blocks, and they should then rise by about 2 ns if block size is the cause.
- The expectation recorded for Exp S in the worklog (section 11.3: '2 MiB blocks are not more expensive than 1 GiB blocks, difference under 1 ns') rests on treating every slot that is 100% RAM as a 1 GiB block. With the VM's pages in slots 0x2080000000 to 0x2280000000, eleven of the fourteen slots were 2 MiB-mapped and at most two were 1 GiB-mapped, so Exp S is not the comparison it was designed to be.
- The list that defines the two classes is not archived. guest-regions.txt (386 region numbers) was written at 19:24 and rewritten by run12 at 19:29 before Exp V2 and W; neither the list nor run12's scan output (vm-frames-n80-run12.txt) is in the repository, so class membership cannot be re-derived. The later scan vm-frames-x.txt shows the VM's footprint still growing slowly (65249 to 65261 pages, 386 to 391 regions), with the three large slots unchanged.
- The evening logs were not all produced by the frames2.c of commit 7ef1be47, as the task text says. By output format and git history: expS.log by the version in 58c45b76 (no class logic), expV.log by 3a0fabf5, expV2.log by 8ec9fe31 (adds the short-working-set loop scaling), expW.log by 58d25d2a (identical to 7ef1be47). The adjacency field counts ascending neighbours only in S, V, V2 and both directions in W.
- Statements in the analyst's documents that go beyond the evidence or are stale at HEAD 145c5318: docs/memlat/README.md:15 and worklog line 26 still say the N80 measurement had no VM; results/memlat-n80-20260930/README.md line 8 states as fact that the host stage-2 table was split into 4 KiB entries, which is inferred from source (this kernel has no host stage-2 dump facility); memlat-rootcause.zh-CN.md:326 states as fact that no VM ran on the N90 and its stage-2 table was not split, which rests on procedure only.
- Noise level to keep in mind for the single-run results (Exp S run 4, Exp V2 with n=2 per size): Exp S contains ten consecutive measurements raised by 4 to 12 ns for about 40 s with L3 refills at 1.00 instead of 0.975; in V2, the two passes of class clear in slot 0x83 at 12 MB differ by 8.4 ns (141.0 and 132.6); at 4 MB the clear class of slot 0x83 is 7.6 ns slower than its shared class. The class effect at 16 MB and above (+13 to +20 ns) is well outside this; the 6 to 12 MB figures (+6 to +9 ns) are not far outside it.
- The 7% to 13% slowdown is a worst case by construction: a random chase where every access misses the TLB and every page's stage-2 descriptor sits on a separate cache line. With contiguous pages it is 3%, and the sequential chase shows no class difference. About 132 thousand host pages (517 MB, roughly 4% of RAM) lay in the 386 regions, so a host workload drawing memory at random would meet the effect on a small share of its pages.
